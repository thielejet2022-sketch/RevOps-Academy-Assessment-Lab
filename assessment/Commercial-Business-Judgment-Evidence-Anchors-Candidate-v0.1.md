# Commercial & Business Judgment — Evidence Anchors

**Status:** Candidate v0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Canonical:** No  
**Parent framework:** RevOps Competency Model Candidate v0.2

## Purpose
Define observable evidence that distinguishes proficiency Levels 1–5 for the **Commercial & Business Judgment** competency.

This Candidate tests whether the five-level proficiency model can produce defensible assessment distinctions without relying on title, confidence, verbosity, or executive vocabulary.

## Competency Definition
Ability to connect RevOps analysis to economics, prioritization, resource allocation, tradeoffs, customer behavior, and business outcomes.

## Scoring Principle
Score the reasoning demonstrated, not the participant's title or presumed experience.

A higher level requires evidence of the lower-level capabilities plus broader scope, stronger integration, better decision judgment, and greater ability to reason under uncertainty.

Do not award a higher level merely because a participant mentions advanced concepts such as CAC, LTV, EBITDA, capacity, unit economics, or capital allocation. The participant must use those concepts correctly and connect them to the facts, hypotheses, decision, or tradeoff at hand.

## Level 1 — Foundational

### Core signal
Recognizes that operating metrics affect business outcomes and can explain straightforward commercial relationships with guidance.

### Observable evidence
The participant can:
- identify whether a metric movement is directionally favorable or unfavorable in context;
- connect a basic funnel or productivity change to a likely revenue consequence;
- distinguish activity from outcome when prompted;
- recognize that resources such as headcount, marketing spend, and technology have costs;
- identify a simple business question that should be answered before acting.

### Example evidence in the funnel scenario
"MQLs are up 29%, but opportunities are down 7%, so the additional top-of-funnel activity is not translating into more sales opportunities. I would want to understand where conversion is breaking before recommending more lead generation."

### Insufficient for this level
- simply restating metrics;
- assuming more activity is automatically better;
- offering a recommendation with no connection to business outcome.

## Level 2 — Practitioner

### Core signal
Uses commercial context to prioritize routine operating decisions and recognizes basic tradeoffs.

### Observable evidence
The participant can:
- connect conversion, productivity, cycle time, or pipeline changes to revenue performance;
- compare at least two plausible actions and explain a practical tradeoff;
- recognize when incremental spend or capacity may not be producing sufficient output;
- identify the evidence needed to choose between near-term alternatives;
- avoid recommending additional investment before understanding whether existing investment is productive.

### Example evidence in the funnel scenario
"Before adding demand spend or more sales capacity, I would determine whether the loss is primarily MQL-to-SQL, SQL-to-opportunity, or AE productivity. If the seven new AEs are still ramping, the response is different than if fully ramped reps lack qualified pipeline."

### Boundary from Level 1
Level 2 does not merely recognize business impact. It uses that impact to prioritize an operating choice.

## Level 3 — Advanced

### Core signal
Integrates multiple operating variables into business judgment and evaluates cross-functional tradeoffs under ambiguity.

### Observable evidence
The participant can:
- connect funnel performance, capacity, productivity, cycle time, and forecast implications rather than analyzing each in isolation;
- frame unit-economic or efficiency consequences even when exact values are unavailable;
- identify second-order effects of an operating decision;
- challenge an assumption embedded in a plan, such as productivity, ramp, conversion, or capacity;
- prioritize investigation according to decision value rather than data availability alone;
- recommend an action path with explicit assumptions, risks, and checkpoints.

### Example evidence in the funnel scenario
"The seven additional AEs increased fixed selling capacity while opportunity creation fell. I would test whether the hiring plan assumed demand and conversion levels that never materialized. If so, the issue is not just funnel conversion; it may also create lower revenue per rep, higher CAC, and forecast risk. I would avoid further capacity additions until we understand productive capacity and qualified pipeline coverage by ramp cohort."

### Boundary from Level 2
Level 3 connects several parts of the revenue system and challenges planning assumptions rather than choosing only among routine operating alternatives.

## Level 4 — Strategic

### Core signal
Shapes cross-functional business decisions by integrating economics, operating-model consequences, resource allocation, and strategic tradeoffs.

### Observable evidence
The participant can:
- evaluate whether resources are allocated coherently across demand generation, qualification, sales capacity, enablement, systems, and other GTM investments;
- connect RevOps evidence to strategic choices and executive priorities;
- distinguish reversible operating experiments from consequential structural decisions;
- assess opportunity cost and the cost of delay;
- identify where incentives, ownership, or governance may be producing economically poor behavior;
- define what evidence is sufficient for an executive decision without demanding perfect information;
- communicate a recommendation in business terms with alternatives and tradeoffs.

### Example evidence in the funnel scenario
"We should not treat this as a request for Marketing to create more leads. We increased both demand activity and sales capacity while opportunity output declined. I would frame an executive decision around where the next dollar of GTM investment belongs. First establish whether the constraint is demand quality, qualification, seller ramp/productivity, or the operating model connecting them. Until that is known, incremental spend risks amplifying an inefficient system."

### Boundary from Level 3
Level 4 uses RevOps evidence to shape cross-functional resource allocation and executive decisions, not only diagnose and improve the operating system.

## Level 5 — Enterprise / Architect

### Core signal
Designs or challenges the economic and operating logic of the revenue system at organizational scale and makes high-leverage decisions under uncertainty.

### Observable evidence
The participant can:
- test whether the organization's growth model coherently connects market opportunity, demand investment, sales capacity, conversion, productivity, cycle time, retention/expansion where relevant, and financial objectives;
- identify structural versus temporary constraints and choose different interventions accordingly;
- reason across multiple time horizons and distinguish short-term revenue protection from long-term operating-model design;
- establish reusable decision principles or governance mechanisms rather than solving only the immediate problem;
- evaluate capital allocation and organizational consequences at enterprise scale;
- identify when a successful playbook from another business would be inappropriate because company economics, GTM motion, stage, or complexity differ;
- make a defensible decision with incomplete information while explicitly managing downside risk and learning speed.

### Example evidence in the funnel scenario
"The immediate funnel deterioration matters, but I would use it to test the growth model itself. We appear to have scaled demand activity and seller capacity without proportional qualified pipeline output. Before treating this as execution variance, I would reconstruct the assumptions that justified those investments: expected channel yield, qualification rates, ramp curves, productivity, pipeline coverage, cycle time, and revenue contribution. If those assumptions are internally inconsistent or no longer true, we need to reallocate capital and reset the operating model, not simply repair a conversion stage. I would separate actions that protect the current quarter from decisions governing the next planning cycle."

### Boundary from Level 4
Level 5 challenges and designs the enterprise growth logic and creates reusable decision architecture. Level 4 makes strong strategic decisions within or across the existing operating model.

## Anti-Inflation Rules
The following do **not** independently justify a higher proficiency score:
- mentioning sophisticated metrics without using them correctly;
- producing more recommendations;
- using executive terminology;
- proposing a transformation;
- confidently asserting causation from incomplete evidence;
- requesting every available metric;
- having held a senior title.

Higher scores require increasingly strong connections among **evidence → interpretation → economics → tradeoff → decision → accountability**.

## Evidence Confidence
A proficiency score should include an evaluator confidence judgment.

Suggested Candidate labels:
- **Low confidence:** isolated or ambiguous evidence;
- **Moderate confidence:** multiple consistent signals but limited breadth or repetition;
- **High confidence:** repeated, clear evidence across materially different situations.

A single exercise may establish demonstrated evidence but should not automatically be treated as a durable competency certification.

## Validation Questions
Before adopting these anchors more broadly, test:
1. Can two evaluators distinguish adjacent levels using the same participant response?
2. Do the anchors reward actual business judgment rather than finance vocabulary?
3. Can a participant demonstrate Level 4 or 5 reasoning without holding a VP title?
4. Can a VP-title participant appropriately score at Level 2 or 3 when evidence supports it?
5. Do company stage, ACV, GTM motion, or ownership model materially change the anchor itself, or only the scenario context?
6. Are Levels 4 and 5 sufficiently distinct to justify retaining both?

## Recommended Next Design Work
Use these anchors to score several intentionally different responses to the same funnel scenario, including at least one response that sounds highly strategic but lacks evidence. This will test evaluator consistency and resistance to executive-language inflation.
