# Commercial & Business Judgment — Evidence Anchors

**Status:** Candidate v0.2  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Canonical:** No  
**Parent framework:** RevOps Competency Model Candidate v0.2  
**Supersedes for testing:** Commercial & Business Judgment Evidence Anchors Candidate v0.1

## Purpose
Provide a usable full-range **L1 through L5** evidence-anchor prototype for one RevOps competency before extending the format across all eleven competency domains.

This version incorporates findings from the adversarial scoring test, path-fairness work, evaluator calibration, three-performance discrimination test, and outer-seam calibration.

## Competency Definition
Ability to connect RevOps analysis to economics, prioritization, resource allocation, tradeoffs, customer behavior, and business outcomes.

## Evaluation Principle
Score demonstrated reasoning, not title, tenure, company size, confidence, vocabulary, or answer length.

Higher proficiency should reflect a change in the **kind of judgment demonstrated**, not merely more concepts or more responsibility.

Evidence chain:

**Evidence → Interpretation → Economics → Tradeoff → Decision → Accountability**

Confidence is recorded separately from proficiency.

---

# L1 — Foundational

## Core signal
**Recognizes commercial consequence and can perform defined reasoning with guidance.**

## Observable evidence
- identifies whether an operating movement is directionally favorable or unfavorable in context;
- connects a straightforward funnel, productivity, or cycle movement to a likely business consequence;
- recognizes that headcount, demand generation, technology, and time carry cost;
- distinguishes activity from outcome when prompted;
- can identify a simple commercial question before action, usually with guidance.

## Boundary test
**Could the participant recognize and explain the commercial issue, but still need help structuring the routine decision?**

If yes, L1 may be appropriate.

## Not enough for L2
Correct terminology, arithmetic, or recognition alone does not demonstrate independent application.

---

# L2 — Practitioner

## Core signal
**Independently applies commercial judgment in familiar operating situations and makes a defensible routine choice.**

## Observable evidence
- independently connects conversion, productivity, cycle, pipeline, or spend to routine revenue consequences;
- compares plausible near-term actions and states a practical tradeoff;
- recognizes when incremental spend or capacity may not be producing sufficient output;
- chooses decision-relevant evidence rather than requesting everything available;
- avoids adding investment before understanding whether existing investment is productive;
- recommends a reasonable routine next step without requiring the evaluator to structure the analysis.

## Boundary from L1
The transition is **independent routine application**, not simply greater knowledge.

## Boundary test
**Did the participant independently use commercial context to choose among familiar operating alternatives?**

---

# L3 — Advanced

## Core signal
**Integrates multiple operating variables and makes sound cross-functional judgments under ambiguity.**

## Observable evidence
- connects funnel performance, capacity, productivity, cycle, forecast, or efficiency rather than treating them independently;
- frames economic consequences even when exact values are unavailable;
- challenges assumptions embedded in hiring, productivity, conversion, capacity, or planning;
- recognizes second-order effects;
- prioritizes investigation by decision value;
- recommends a bounded action path with assumptions, risks, checkpoints, and owners;
- updates the recommendation when evidence changes.

## Boundary from L2
L3 moves beyond familiar alternatives and handles **ambiguity, interaction, and planning assumptions**.

## Boundary test
**Did the participant integrate several parts of the revenue system into a defensible response to an ambiguous business problem?**

---

# L4 — Strategic

## Core signal
**Uses RevOps evidence to shape material cross-functional resource decisions and the operating mechanisms around those decisions.**

## Observable evidence
- evaluates allocation across demand, qualification, selling capacity, enablement, systems, or other GTM investments;
- explicitly weighs opportunity cost, reversibility, economic consequence, and cost of delay;
- makes or frames a material resource decision before perfect certainty when sufficient evidence exists;
- distinguishes reversible experiments from structural decisions;
- identifies incentives, ownership, planning assumptions, or governance that produce economically poor behavior;
- changes an operating standard, investment gate, executive decision frame, or cross-functional governance mechanism;
- states what should **not** change yet when evidence does not support it.

## Boundary from L3
L3 diagnoses and designs a strong response to the operating system. L4 **materially shapes the system's decisions, tradeoffs, standards, governance, or senior-stakeholder action**.

## Boundary test
**Did the participant use the economics to allocate or constrain resources and change how consequential decisions are made?**

---

# L5 — Enterprise / Architect

## Core signal
**Creates reusable commercial decision architecture that remains coherent across materially different contexts, complexity, scale, and change.**

## Observable evidence
- abstracts durable commercial principles from a local solution without assuming the local thresholds or playbook are universal;
- distinguishes which decision rules should be common and which parameters should vary by GTM motion, product, geography, business unit, portfolio company, stage, or economic model;
- establishes governance for exceptions, local autonomy, model evolution, and controlled variation;
- designs comparable economic decision logic across different operating environments;
- reasons across multiple time horizons and materially different business contexts;
- governs complexity rather than forcing artificial uniformity;
- demonstrates how the decision architecture adapts when assumptions, markets, organizational structures, or growth models change;
- makes high-leverage decisions under uncertainty while protecting downside and learning speed.

## Boundary from L4
L4 strategically shapes a complex operating system. L5 **creates reusable architecture and governance that can transfer across materially different operating contexts without losing integrity**.

## Boundary test
**Did the participant merely design an excellent strategic solution for this organization, or did they demonstrate how the commercial decision architecture transfers, varies, and remains governed across different contexts?**

## Critical anti-bias rule
L5 is **not organizational size**.

Do not award L5 merely because the participant:
- works at a large enterprise;
- supports multiple teams or business units;
- manages a large budget;
- holds a VP/CRO/Operating Partner title;
- repeats the same playbook in several companies;
- uses enterprise-transformation language.

A smaller-company operator may demonstrate L5-quality architecture if the evidence genuinely shows transferability and complexity governance. A Fortune 100 executive may demonstrate only L3 or L4 in this competency if that is what the evidence supports.

# Cross-Cutting Scoring Rules

## Healthy uncertainty
Positive evidence includes identifying material unknowns, asking questions that could change the decision, and avoiding unsupported irreversible action.

Healthy uncertainty stops being positive when the participant continues analysis after enough evidence exists for a proportional, reversible decision.

## Hypothesis revision
Changing a hypothesis when evidence contradicts it is positive evidence. Consistency is not competence when the facts changed.

## Unsupported certainty
Confident causation without adequate evidence should limit proficiency regardless of decisiveness or seniority.

## No keyword scoring
CAC, LTV, ARR, EBITDA, capital allocation, transformation, governance, operating model, and similar language carry no independent scoring value.

## Hidden evidence
Do not penalize participants for evidence they were never shown and had no reasonable basis to request. Score the reasoning against encountered evidence.

## Insufficient evidence
Use **Insufficient evidence** when the exercise does not expose enough behavior to support a level. Do not force a low score merely because a competency was not tested.

## Confidence
Record Low, Moderate, or High confidence separately from proficiency. A single exercise may show strong evidence without establishing durable professional capability.

# Exercise Design Implication

A single scenario can support multiple proficiency levels if the evaluator changes what evidence is expected, not merely the numerical difficulty.

For Commercial & Business Judgment:
- L1 can be demonstrated through recognizing commercial consequence;
- L2 through independent routine tradeoff decisions;
- L3 through ambiguous cross-functional integration;
- L4 through material resource allocation and operating-model decisions;
- L5 requires evidence of reusable architecture and complexity governance.

Because L5 may be difficult to observe credibly in one bounded scenario, some L5 assessments may require multiple contrasting contexts or work-history evidence. Do not manufacture artificial “enterprise” complexity merely to force an L5 test.

# Prototype Validation Result

This full-range format is conceptually usable for Commercial & Business Judgment and incorporates the currently tested adjacent-level distinctions.

It does **not** yet prove that the same format can be copied mechanically to all other competencies. In particular:
- Systems & Automation may express L5 through architecture/governance differently;
- Communication may express L5 through enterprise decision framing and reusable communication systems;
- Change Management may require evidence of adoption across materially different stakeholder environments;
- Data & Analytics may require reusable measurement/decision architecture rather than broader reporting scale.

Therefore this document should serve as a **prototype pattern**, not a template to mass-produce without competency-specific testing.

## Next Validation Question
Before extending the format to all eleven competencies, test it on one competency with a materially different character. **Systems & Automation** is the strongest contrast because the model explicitly distinguishes personal configuration skill from architecture and governance responsibility.

If the five-level structure transfers cleanly to both Commercial & Business Judgment and Systems & Automation, confidence in the general proficiency architecture increases substantially.
