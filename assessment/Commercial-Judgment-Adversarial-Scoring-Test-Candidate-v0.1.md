# Commercial Judgment — Adversarial Scoring Test

**Status:** Candidate v0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Canonical:** No

## Purpose
Stress-test the Commercial & Business Judgment Evidence Anchors Candidate v0.1 using intentionally different responses to the same funnel-deterioration scenario.

The test is designed to determine whether the evidence anchors can distinguish genuine business judgment from polished language, title signaling, excessive confidence, or sophisticated vocabulary.

## Shared Scenario
A B2B SaaS company reports:
- MQL volume +29%
- SQL volume +5%
- Sales headcount +25% through 7 additional AEs
- Opportunities created -7%
- Average sales cycle increased to 82 days

No additional facts are initially available.

---

## Response A — Operationally Competent

> The first issue I would isolate is where the conversion loss occurs. MQL growth is not carrying through to SQLs, and SQL growth is not carrying through to opportunities. I would segment MQL-to-SQL and SQL-to-opportunity conversion by source, segment, cohort, and time period. I would also separate the seven added AEs by ramp status because fully ramped reps without enough qualified pipeline would suggest a different problem from normal ramp dilution. I would not recommend adding more leads or sellers until we know which constraint is dominant.

### Scoring
**Commercial & Business Judgment: Level 2 — Practitioner**  
**Confidence: High for this exercise**

### Evidence
The response connects funnel performance to an operating choice, recognizes a capacity tradeoff, requests decision-relevant evidence, and avoids incremental investment before understanding productivity.

### Why not Level 3
It does not materially connect the problem to broader economics, forecast implications, planning assumptions, second-order effects, or cross-functional resource tradeoffs.

---

## Response B — Integrated Business Judgment

> Seven additional AEs increased selling capacity by 25% while opportunity creation declined 7%. I would test whether the hiring plan assumed demand, conversion, and ramp productivity that did not materialize. If that is true, this is more than a lead-quality issue: revenue per rep may deteriorate, acquisition efficiency may worsen, and the forecast may be relying on capacity that is not actually productive. I would freeze further capacity expansion while we segment conversion and pipeline coverage by source and rep-ramp cohort, then revisit the capacity plan using observed productivity rather than the original assumption.

### Scoring
**Commercial & Business Judgment: Level 3 — Advanced**  
**Confidence: High for this exercise**

### Evidence
The response integrates funnel, capacity, ramp, productivity, acquisition efficiency, and forecast consequences; challenges a planning assumption; and proposes a decision path with an explicit interim action.

### Why not Level 4
It remains primarily focused on diagnosing and correcting the current operating plan. It does not yet frame broader executive resource allocation, opportunity cost, governance, or strategic alternatives across the GTM system.

---

## Response C — Strategic but Grounded

> I would frame this for the executive team as a capital-allocation question, not a Marketing-versus-Sales problem. We increased both demand activity and sales capacity while qualified opportunity output declined. Before committing the next dollar, we need to know whether the binding constraint is channel quality, qualification, seller ramp/productivity, or the operating model connecting them. I would separate reversible near-term experiments from structural decisions. In the short term, protect pipeline generation and diagnose conversion by channel and ramp cohort. For the next planning cycle, revisit the assumptions that determine how much we invest in demand, qualification, and sales capacity. The decision is not whether MQLs are good or bad; it is where incremental GTM investment produces the highest economic return.

### Scoring
**Commercial & Business Judgment: Level 4 — Strategic**  
**Confidence: High for this exercise**

### Evidence
The response translates RevOps evidence into executive resource allocation, distinguishes reversible actions from structural decisions, connects the problem to investment efficiency, and defines a strategic decision without pretending the current evidence proves causation.

### Why not Level 5
It challenges resource allocation within and across the current operating model but does not yet reconstruct or redesign the enterprise growth logic, establish reusable governance, or reason explicitly across organizational scale and multiple planning horizons.

---

## Response D — Enterprise / Architect

> I would treat the deterioration as a trigger to test whether the growth model is internally coherent. The company scaled top-of-funnel activity and seller capacity, but qualified pipeline output moved in the opposite direction. I would reconstruct the assumptions that justified those investments: market and channel yield, qualification rates, ramp curves, productive capacity, pipeline coverage, conversion, cycle time, and expected revenue contribution. We need to determine whether this is temporary execution variance or evidence that the model used to allocate GTM capital is no longer valid. I would protect the current quarter with targeted conversion and ramp interventions while separately resetting the next planning cycle if the structural assumptions fail. The durable output should not just be a fix to this funnel; it should be a decision framework that tells leadership when additional demand or headcount is economically justified and what evidence must exist before that capital is committed.

### Scoring
**Commercial & Business Judgment: Level 5 — Enterprise / Architect**  
**Confidence: High for this exercise**

### Evidence
The response tests enterprise growth logic, distinguishes temporary from structural constraints, reasons across time horizons, connects analysis to capital allocation, and proposes reusable decision architecture rather than only solving the immediate problem.

---

## Response E — The Executive-Language Trap

> This is clearly a strategic RevOps transformation issue. We need to optimize the end-to-end GTM flywheel, align stakeholders around a unified source of truth, leverage AI-driven insights, rationalize the tech stack, improve CAC and LTV, implement best-in-class pipeline governance, and create an executive steering committee. I would build a 30-60-90 day transformation roadmap with clear KPIs and hold Marketing and Sales accountable to world-class conversion benchmarks. The organization needs to move from reactive RevOps to proactive, data-driven revenue orchestration.

### Scoring
**Commercial & Business Judgment: Level 1 — Foundational evidence at best**  
**Confidence: Moderate**

### Evidence
The response recognizes that the situation may have business implications, but it does not use the supplied facts to establish what is happening, identify the decision-relevant uncertainty, evaluate a tradeoff, or connect a proposed action to demonstrated economics.

### Why the language does not raise the score
The response uses advanced vocabulary but provides almost no defensible chain from evidence to interpretation to economics to tradeoff to decision to accountability. It asserts a transformation without establishing whether the problem is structural.

---

## Response F — Confident but Unsupported Causation

> Marketing quality has obviously collapsed. The 29% increase in MQLs produced only 5% more SQLs, so the company should cut low-performing marketing channels immediately and shift budget into outbound. The new AEs are not the issue because they have more leads available. The 82-day sales cycle confirms that poor-fit inbound leads are clogging the pipeline.

### Scoring
**Commercial & Business Judgment: Below Level 2 / weak Level 1 evidence**  
**Confidence: High**

### Evidence
The response notices a meaningful conversion signal, but it converts correlation into causation, dismisses alternative explanations without evidence, and recommends resource reallocation before establishing channel performance or AE ramp/productivity.

### Important finding
Confidence is not competence. A decisive recommendation based on unsupported causal claims should score below a more cautious response that correctly identifies uncertainty and asks for decision-relevant evidence.

---

## Stress-Test Findings

### 1. The five levels remain useful
The examples can be distinguished by the scope and quality of demonstrated reasoning rather than by title or vocabulary. Levels 4 and 5 remain meaningfully different: Level 4 shapes strategic decisions and resource allocation; Level 5 tests and designs the enterprise decision architecture itself.

### 2. Anti-inflation rules work conceptually
Response E demonstrates why sophisticated language must not substitute for evidence. A scoring system that rewards keyword density would badly overrate this response.

### 3. Unsupported certainty should be penalized
Response F shows that recognizing a plausible hypothesis is not enough. The model should explicitly penalize unsupported causal certainty when the participant had insufficient evidence.

### 4. Good uncertainty handling is positive evidence
Knowing what is not yet known, identifying the smallest useful next evidence, and delaying irreversible decisions can itself demonstrate business judgment.

### 5. Scoring needs both level and confidence
One scenario can provide strong evidence about the reasoning displayed in that scenario, but should not automatically establish durable proficiency across contexts.

## Candidate Refinements Suggested
Future rubric versions should explicitly evaluate:
- evidence discipline;
- causal discipline;
- decision quality;
- tradeoff quality;
- uncertainty handling;
- economic connection;
- accountability/implementation logic.

These may become evidence dimensions within a competency rather than separate competencies.
