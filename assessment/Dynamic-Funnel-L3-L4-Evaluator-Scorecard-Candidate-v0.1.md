# Dynamic Funnel Diagnosis — L3/L4 Evaluator Scorecard

**Status:** Candidate v0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Canonical:** No  
**Target use:** Evaluator calibration for Dynamic Funnel Diagnosis  
**Primary target profile:** VP of Revenue Operations  
**Related artifacts:** RevOps Competency Model Candidate v0.2; Scoring Architecture Candidate v0.1; Evaluator Consistency Test Candidate v0.1

## Purpose
Reduce evaluator discretion at the boundary between **Level 3 — Advanced** and **Level 4 — Strategic** without converting a dynamic assessment into a checklist or rewarding scripted executive language.

This scorecard applies only to competencies meaningfully observable in the Dynamic Funnel Diagnosis exercise. It does not redefine the universal competency model.

## Core Calibration Principle
The practical L3→L4 transition is generally:

> **L3 diagnoses and designs a strong response to the system; L4 materially shapes the system, its decisions, tradeoffs, standards, governance, or senior-stakeholder action.**

This is a calibration heuristic, not a universal sentence-completion rule. Evidence must remain competency-specific.

## Scoring Method
For each competency:
1. identify observable participant evidence;
2. compare it with the L3 and L4 anchors below;
3. assign L3, L4, another supported level, or **Insufficient evidence**;
4. record confidence separately;
5. do not average adjacent levels when evidence is genuinely mixed.

A participant may show L4-quality evidence handling but L3 decision execution. Preserve that distinction in evaluator notes.

# 1. GTM Systems Thinking

### L3 — Advanced anchor
Integrates multiple GTM mechanisms rather than treating metrics in isolation. Connects channel, qualification, sales capacity, segment, cycle, or economics into a coherent diagnosis. Recognizes interactions and second-order effects.

### L4 — Strategic anchor
Uses the integrated diagnosis to challenge or shape the cross-functional operating model. Identifies a material system-level decision, incentive, planning assumption, ownership gap, or governance mechanism that must change.

### L4 is not earned merely by
- saying “end-to-end funnel”;
- naming every function;
- requesting more cross-functional data;
- describing the problem as systemic without changing a system-level decision or mechanism.

### Typical boundary question
**Did the participant only connect the system, or did they use that connection to shape how the system should operate?**

---

# 2. Funnel & Lifecycle Management

### L3 — Advanced anchor
Diagnoses ambiguous conversion deterioration across stages, segments, cohorts, or channels; distinguishes volume from quality; challenges stage integrity or qualification assumptions; identifies high-value follow-up evidence.

### L4 — Strategic anchor
Converts the diagnosis into a material cross-functional funnel decision or operating standard, such as changing investment gates, qualification governance, lifecycle accountability, cohort review, or the criteria used to scale a channel or segment.

### L4 is not earned merely by
- finding the worst conversion rate;
- identifying the correct channel or segment;
- proposing generic funnel optimization;
- using strategic language without a consequential operating choice.

### Typical boundary question
**Did the participant diagnose where/why yield deteriorated, or did they also change how the company will govern and act on funnel yield?**

---

# 3. Data & Analytics

### L3 — Advanced anchor
Frames ambiguous analysis correctly, decomposes aggregate metrics, distinguishes fact from hypothesis, requests decision-relevant evidence, notices denominator/mix/cohort problems, and avoids unsupported causation.

### L4 — Strategic anchor
Uses analytical framing to change a consequential business decision, measurement standard, planning assumption, or executive interpretation. Establishes what evidence is sufficient for action and what should remain uncertain.

### L4 is not earned merely by
- asking sophisticated questions;
- requesting more granular dashboards;
- naming statistical or analytical concepts;
- refusing to decide until every uncertainty is resolved.

### Typical boundary question
**Did the analysis improve understanding, or did it materially change the decision architecture?**

---

# 4. Commercial & Business Judgment

### L3 — Advanced anchor
Connects operating evidence to revenue, productivity, unit economics, planning assumptions, and cross-functional tradeoffs. Challenges whether increased activity is economically productive. Prioritizes consequential uncertainty.

### L4 — Strategic anchor
Makes or frames a material resource-allocation decision under uncertainty. Explicitly weighs opportunity cost, reversibility, economic consequence, and what should or should not receive the next incremental dollar or unit of capacity.

### L4 is not earned merely by
- mentioning CAC, LTV, ARR, EBITDA, efficiency, or capital allocation;
- noting that economics worsened;
- recommending more analysis without stating what investment decision can responsibly be made now.

### Typical boundary question
**Did the participant understand the economics, or did they use the economics to allocate or constrain resources?**

---

# 5. Operating Cadence & Execution

### L3 — Advanced anchor
Defines a practical corrective operating rhythm with owners, checkpoints, decision metrics, and follow-through across affected teams. Turns diagnosis into coordinated execution rather than a one-time analysis.

### L4 — Strategic anchor
Establishes or materially redesigns an executive operating cadence or governance mechanism that changes how cross-functional performance and investment decisions are inspected over time.

### L4 is not earned merely by
- proposing a weekly meeting;
- saying “create accountability”;
- assigning owners without defining what decisions the cadence enables;
- adding reporting burden without changing behavior or decisions.

### Typical boundary question
**Did the participant organize execution, or did they change the management system through which executives inspect and govern the revenue engine?**

---

# 6. Communication & Executive Readout

### L3 — Advanced anchor
Explains the situation clearly, separates fact from hypothesis, prioritizes material evidence, identifies unresolved questions, and gives an audience-appropriate recommendation without unnecessary analytical detail.

### L4 — Strategic anchor
Frames the executive decision crisply: what is happening, what is known versus uncertain, what should be done now, what should not be done yet, the business tradeoff, and who/what must change at operating-model level.

### L4 is not earned merely by
- polished language;
- confidence;
- brevity alone;
- executive vocabulary;
- a dramatic recommendation unsupported by evidence.

### Typical boundary question
**Did the participant summarize the analysis, or did they enable an executive team to make and own the decision?**

# Cross-Cutting Calibration Rules

## Healthy uncertainty vs analysis paralysis
Positive evidence:
- identifies material unknowns;
- requests evidence that could change a decision;
- avoids irreversible action when causation is weak;
- chooses a bounded/reversible action when sufficient evidence exists.

Negative evidence:
- continues analysis after enough evidence exists for a reversible decision;
- treats perfect certainty as a prerequisite for action;
- uses “need more data” without specifying what decision the data will change.

## Hypothesis revision
Changing a hypothesis after contradictory evidence is **positive evidence**, especially for Data & Analytics and Causal/Uncertainty lenses. Do not reward consistency for its own sake.

## Hidden evidence
Do not penalize a participant for failing to mention facts they were never shown. Score claims against encountered evidence. Mark unobservable competencies **Insufficient evidence**.

## Decision responsibility
At VP altitude, the evaluator should look for at least one material decision or explicit decision frame when the encountered evidence reasonably supports it. The decision may be reversible and provisional.

Examples include:
- holding incremental channel spend while preserving productive demand sources;
- pausing incremental hiring until productive-capacity assumptions are rebuilt;
- resetting plan assumptions;
- establishing end-to-end funnel economics as an investment gate;
- changing executive ownership or review cadence.

The participant does not need to choose any specific example to demonstrate L4.

## No keyword scoring
Terms such as “capital allocation,” “governance,” “operating model,” “CAC,” or “transformation” carry no independent scoring value. The evaluator must identify the actual reasoning and consequence behind the language.

# Rescoring Check — Live VP Transcript

Using these anchors, the first live transcript should provisionally read:

- GTM Systems Thinking: **L3 Advanced**, Moderate-High confidence
- Funnel & Lifecycle Management: **L3 Advanced**, High confidence
- Data & Analytics: **L4 Strategic evidence in analytical framing, but overall competency provisional L3-L4 boundary**, Moderate confidence
- Commercial & Business Judgment: **L3 Advanced**, High confidence
- Operating Cadence & Execution: **L2 / insufficient L3 evidence**, Low confidence
- Communication & Executive Readout: **L3 Advanced**, Moderate confidence
- VP Role Fit: **Approaching target evidence**

The scorecard narrows the prior evaluator disagreement by making the missing strategic behavior explicit: the participant did not yet convert strong diagnosis into a material cross-functional decision, resource tradeoff, or durable operating mechanism.

This is a calibration result, not a durable judgment of the participant's overall professional capability.

---

# Contrasting Transcript Control

A contrasting VP response using the same evidence should qualify for stronger L4 consideration if it:
- preserves fact/hypothesis discipline;
- makes a bounded resource-allocation decision;
- explicitly states what it will not change yet;
- connects channel, segment, productive capacity, economics, and planning assumptions where supported;
- creates a durable decision/governance mechanism rather than only another analysis request.

A polished response that merely mentions these concepts without showing the evidence-to-decision chain should remain below L4.

## Validation Questions
Before this scorecard is promoted or incorporated into a broader evaluator standard, test:
1. whether two genuinely independent evaluators use these anchors consistently;
2. whether the anchors reduce L3/L4 disagreement without suppressing valid alternative reasoning paths;
3. whether the L4 anchors accidentally over-reward action bias;
4. whether the scorecard works on weak, average, and exceptional transcripts;
5. whether participants with different functional backgrounds can demonstrate equivalent quality through different evidence paths;
6. whether the scorecard remains usable without the designer present.
