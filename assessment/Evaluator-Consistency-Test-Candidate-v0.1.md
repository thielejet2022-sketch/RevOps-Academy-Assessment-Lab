# Evaluator Consistency Test

**Status:** Candidate v0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Canonical:** No  
**Target profile:** VP of Revenue Operations  
**Related artifacts:** `competency-model/RevOps-Competency-Model-Candidate-v0.2.md`; `assessment/Scoring-Architecture-Candidate-v0.1.md`; `assessment/Dynamic-Path-Fairness-Pressure-Test-Candidate-v0.1.md`

## Purpose
Test whether two evaluator passes can reach reasonably consistent judgments from the same participant evidence without relying on a preferred hidden-evidence sequence.

This is a **pseudo-independent calibration test**, not empirical inter-rater validation. Both passes are intentionally separated in logic and compared only afterward, but they are not two independent human raters. The artifact exists to expose scoring ambiguity before a real two-rater study.

# Fixed Participant Evidence

The participant completed the first live VP-altitude Dynamic Funnel Diagnosis prototype.

## Stage 0 response
The participant immediately identified:
- MQL volume increased 29% while SQL increased only 5%, signaling a major conversion issue;
- 7 added AEs did not reconcile with opportunities falling 7%;
- rep behavior should be examined by individual and cohort to distinguish hiring versus training/system issues;
- the aggregate sales-cycle increase needed decomposition by stage and magnitude;
- ASP could materially change the interpretation.

The participant then prioritized actual revenue output over activity metrics, asking how much revenue was generated and arguing that opportunity decline is more or less grave depending on economic output.

## Economics response
After learning Marketing spend +34%, paid-social spend +96%, cost per created opportunity +44%, Sales payroll +21%, and New ARR +3%, the participant:
- hypothesized that increased Marketing investment was not translating proportionally to closed/won revenue;
- asked for conversion rates using consistent definitions;
- identified cost per opportunity as concerning;
- wanted CAC and spend-versus-results by channel;
- requested revenue per AE to distinguish individual performance from a systemic issue.

## AE capacity response
After learning that only 2 of 7 added AEs were fully ramped, tenured conversion was flat, newer AEs created 18% fewer qualified opportunities per SQL, and enablement capacity had not increased, the participant pivoted back to the top-of-funnel drop and requested channel conversion rates.

## Channel response
After learning paid-social MQLs +88%, paid-social MQL-to-SQL fell 11% to 4%, while Organic and Partner/Referral conversion stayed nearly flat, the participant concluded that paid social was a materially different pattern and asked where sales-cycle duration changed by stage.

## Sales-cycle response
After learning Enterprise cycle 101→108 days, Mid-market 69→72, SMB 41→61, and the largest increase was Discovery→Qualified Evaluation, the participant:
- hypothesized that paid-social growth may have increased SMB volume;
- hypothesized that discovery support, demos, Q&A, or enablement constraints could contribute to the delay;
- asked for segment breakdown to connect channel and cycle behavior;
- also asked how revenue compared with plan.

## Segment response
After learning SMB SQL-to-opportunity fell 29%→18%, SMB became 41% of SQL volume from 30%, and paid social disproportionately contributed to SMB, the participant's executive summary stated:
- the company hyper-invested in paid social and generated more MQLs that were not becoming sales-qualified;
- SMB sales cycle increased by 20 days;
- SMB conversion evidence was concerning;
- more analysis was needed to determine why SMB leads were not converting and why SQLs were taking longer to close.

The final recommendation stopped short of a concrete resource-allocation or governance action and concluded that the company should dig further into lead quality and sales-cycle causes.

# Comparison

## Where the evaluators agree
Both passes agree that:
- the participant demonstrated strong Evidence Discipline and Causal Discipline;
- the participant showed meaningful economic instincts;
- the participant operated at least at Advanced level in core diagnostic work;
- Data & Analytics was among the strongest observed competencies;
- several competencies were genuinely **Insufficient evidence** and should not be forced to a low score;
- final VP role fit was **Approaching target evidence**;
- the main gap was not diagnosis but conversion of evidence into executive decision, accountability, and operating-model action.

## Where the evaluators differ
The largest disagreement is Funnel & Lifecycle Management:
- Pass A: L4 Strategic
- Pass B: L3 Advanced

The difference comes from what counts as sufficient evidence of strategic proficiency. Pass A gives more credit to the participant's inquiry architecture and integration across channel, segment, capacity, and cycle. Pass B requires the participant to convert that diagnosis into a cross-functional strategic operating decision before assigning L4.

A smaller disagreement exists in Process Design & Optimization and Cross-Functional Leadership because the transcript contains hypotheses and questions about system causes but little demonstrated implementation or stakeholder action.

# Calibration Finding

## Finding 1 — Proficiency anchors must distinguish diagnosis from decision
The current five-level descriptions are too broad to guarantee consistent scoring in dynamic executive exercises.

For competencies such as Funnel & Lifecycle Management and Commercial & Business Judgment, evaluator guidance should explicitly distinguish:
- **L3 Advanced:** integrates ambiguous evidence, diagnoses cross-functional causes, challenges assumptions, and connects to business outcomes;
- **L4 Strategic:** additionally converts that diagnosis into a material cross-functional operating decision, tradeoff, standard, governance mechanism, or senior-stakeholder action appropriate to the competency.

This should not become a universal rule that every L4 answer must include an action sentence. It is a calibration anchor for situations where the target competency inherently includes strategic decision responsibility.

## Finding 2 — Inquiry quality and final decision should be scored separately
A participant can demonstrate L4-quality evidence handling while only L3 decision execution in the same exercise. The scoring record should preserve that distinction rather than allowing a strong inquiry path to automatically elevate the entire competency.

## Finding 3 — Role fit is more stable than individual competency scores
Despite level disagreements, both evaluators independently converge on **Approaching target evidence** for the VP profile. This suggests role-fit comparison may be somewhat more robust than precise adjacent-level scoring, but this must be tested with real independent evaluators.

## Finding 4 — Low-observation competencies should remain unscored
The temptation to infer Cross-Functional Leadership, Change Management, or Systems capability from a polished diagnostic answer creates artificial precision. `Insufficient evidence` is functioning as an important fairness mechanism.

## Finding 5 — Evaluator consistency is not yet validated
This pseudo-independent test surfaced a plausible L3/L4 boundary disagreement. A real validation step requires two genuinely independent evaluators using the same transcript and anchors without seeing each other's scores.

# Candidate Calibration Rule

When adjacent proficiency judgments differ because one evaluator rewards **diagnostic sophistication** and another requires **decision responsibility**, evaluators should ask:

> What observable behavior in this competency demonstrates the transition from understanding and diagnosing the system to shaping the system?

The answer should be competency-specific and grounded in the participant's actual evidence.

Do not resolve disagreement by averaging levels.
