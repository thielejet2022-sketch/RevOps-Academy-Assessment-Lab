# Forecast, Capacity & Revenue Planning — End-to-End System Validation Run

**Status:** Candidate v0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Canonical:** No  
**Instrument tested:** Forecast, Capacity & Revenue Planning Assessment Instrument Candidate v0.1  
**Dataset:** RP-001-v0.1 embedded Candidate dataset  
**Target Role Profile:** VP Revenue Operations  
**Participant:** Controlled synthetic participant  
**Validation purpose:** Test whether A2 distinguishes integrated planning from strategic planning governance and whether its evidence accumulates cleanly with the broader methodology.

## Important Limitation
This is a synthetic system test authored and evaluated within the same development environment. It is not independent evaluator evidence, inter-rater reliability evidence, predictive validity, or hiring validity.

---

# Synthetic Participant Design

The participant is deliberately modeled as a **strong Director-level RevOps operator attempting VP-altitude planning work**.

Intended behavior:
- strong quantitative and planning discipline;
- challenges obviously weak assumptions;
- integrates funnel, ramp, capacity, and timing;
- builds a practical plan;
- does not naturally redesign executive planning governance or establish strong capital-release decision rules unless the evidence compels it;
- communicates clearly without relying on executive vocabulary.

This participant is intentionally not weak. The test is whether competent L3 work remains L3 rather than inflating to L4 because the response is polished and numerate.

# Interaction Trace

## Stage 0
Participant receives the baseline: $12M current New ARR, $15M preliminary target, 24 AEs / 20 fully ramped, $600K fully ramped productivity, 22% win rate, $30K ACV, $54.5M qualified pipeline, +30% marketing-spend proposal, eight proposed AEs, and a four-month finance ramp assumption.

### Participant response
The participant says the $15M target should be treated as an objective rather than yet as a credible operating forecast. They want to decompose the target into required qualified pipeline, seller capacity, ramp timing, productivity, conversion, and ACV assumptions.

They ask for:
1. current attainment and historical growth;
2. pipeline required at current yield;
3. historical AE ramp and productivity distribution;
4. realistic hiring dates.

### Evidence released
Packets A, B, C, D, E.

### Observed reasoning
The participant immediately identifies several planning issues:
- actual growth is approximately 15%, so 25% requires acceleration;
- the company missed the current $13M plan;
- $15M at current pipeline yield requires about $68.2M qualified pipeline, roughly 25% more than current;
- Finance's four-month ramp is contradicted by 8–9 month historical ramp;
- $650K planned productivity is above both the current $600K average and $560K median;
- hiring is staggered and attrition is unmodeled.

They state that simply multiplying eight AEs by $650K would materially overstate next-year capacity.

**Provisional evidence:** strong L3 Forecasting & Revenue Planning and Data & Analytics.

---

## Stage 1 — Demand and conversion assumptions
The participant asks what supports the assumed 30% pipeline increase and whether management is also assuming conversion or ACV improvement.

### Evidence released
Packets F and G.

### Participant update
They identify an **optimism stack**:
- +30% marketing spend is assumed to produce +30% qualified pipeline despite prior +22% spend producing only +13% pipeline;
- win rate is assumed to improve 22% → 24% without a defined initiative;
- ACV is assumed to improve $30K → $32K without approved pricing or completed segment redesign;
- fully ramped productivity is assumed to improve to $650K;
- ramp is assumed to compress to four months.

They say any one improvement might be achievable, but the plan should not depend on all of them simultaneously without named initiatives, owners, timing, and evidence.

They propose three planning scenarios:
- **Base:** largely current conversion/ACV/productivity with historical ramp;
- **Operating improvement:** selected improvements tied to explicit initiatives;
- **Upside:** $15M if leading assumptions demonstrate evidence early enough.

**Provisional evidence:** L3 integrated planning; strong uncertainty handling; no L4 evidence yet.

---

## Stage 2 — Timing and economics
Participant asks for seasonality, acquisition economics, enablement/management constraints, and executive decision constraints.

### Evidence released
Packets H, I, J, L.

### Participant update
They reject an evenly spread quarterly target because historical seasonality and pipeline maturity make Q1 structurally smaller. They also note that most incremental AE capacity arrives after Q1 and becomes materially productive later because of historical ramp.

They flag that acquisition spending would rise approximately 28% before management/enablement additions while CAC is already up 17% and ACV only 2%.

They recommend:
- not authorizing all eight AEs as if the plan were already proven;
- authorize recruiting immediately because lead time matters;
- authorize the first hiring tranche;
- preserve the ability to add later cohorts;
- require Marketing to separate committed spend from experimental/incremental spend;
- add enablement capacity analysis before onboarding the full cohort;
- use a seasonally phased plan rather than equal quarterly targets.

They do **not** yet define an explicit cross-functional governance mechanism for assumption ownership or capital release.

**Provisional evidence:** high L3 Commercial Judgment; planning remains L3 because resource staging is present but governance is still operational rather than strategic/system-shaping.

# Required Planning Decision Gate

Participant is asked to present the recommended operating plan to CEO/CRO/CMO/CFO.

## Synthetic participant decision

### Revenue commitment
The participant recommends:
- **Committed operating plan:** $14.2M New ARR
- **Upside objective:** $15.0M

They explain that $15M should remain strategically visible but should not be the operating commitment until the organization demonstrates enough pipeline creation and productivity improvement to support it.

### Assumptions
Base commitment assumes:
- historical 8–9 month ramp rather than four months;
- no automatic $650K productivity assumption for every fully ramped AE;
- no automatic 24% win rate or $32K ACV;
- seasonally phased bookings;
- attrition included in capacity planning;
- incremental pipeline improvement below the proposed 30% until channel plans demonstrate otherwise.

### Resources
Recommend:
- begin recruiting now;
- authorize first two AE hires and prepare the March cohort, but hold the final three May hires as conditional;
- fund the core marketing plan plus a bounded incremental test budget rather than releasing the entire +30% as committed spend;
- assess frontline-management and enablement capacity before later AE cohorts arrive.

### What not to assume
Do not assume:
- four-month full ramp;
- linear marketing-spend-to-pipeline response;
- simultaneous win-rate, ACV, productivity, and ramp improvements;
- that more seller capacity fixes insufficient pipeline.

### Operating management
Participant proposes monthly reforecasting with weekly leading-indicator review of:
- qualified pipeline creation versus phased plan;
- pipeline by segment/source;
- ramp cohort productivity;
- win rate;
- ACV;
- CAC / cost per qualified opportunity;
- hiring/onboarding milestones.

They say the May hiring cohort should be released only if Q1/Q2 leading indicators show enough demand and productive capacity to justify it.

### Evaluator observation
This is a strong, bounded, economically aware operating plan. The participant makes resource decisions and creates a conditional hiring gate.

However, the response stops short of a fuller L4 planning-governance design. It does not clearly establish:
- executive ownership of material assumptions;
- a recurring cross-functional mechanism for approving assumption changes;
- explicit decision rights between Finance, Sales, Marketing, and RevOps;
- a durable planning standard distinguishing target, commitment, forecast, and upside across the company.

**Forecasting & Revenue Planning:** L3 Advanced, near L4 boundary.

# Planning Shock

Participant receives:

> At the end of Q1, qualified pipeline creation is 12% below planning assumption, but fully ramped AE productivity is 6% above plan. CRO wants to accelerate remaining AE hires because productivity looks strong. CFO wants to freeze hiring because pipeline is behind.

## Synthetic participant response
The participant says neither executive's single metric is sufficient.

They interpret the evidence as:
- existing productive capacity is performing better than assumed;
- demand/pipeline creation is currently the tighter constraint;
- accelerating all remaining AE hiring could add capacity before sufficient opportunity volume exists;
- freezing all hiring could be premature because recruiting/ramp lead times make capacity slow to recover if pipeline rebounds.

Recommendation:
- continue recruiting so the option remains open;
- do not accelerate the full remaining cohort;
- hold the May start-date commitment temporarily;
- diagnose the 12% pipeline shortfall by source, segment, and timing;
- compare current pipeline coverage against the next two quarters' productive capacity;
- set a near-term decision point for the May cohort based on pipeline recovery and expected capacity coverage.

The participant updates the plan rather than defending the original forecast.

### Evaluator observation
This is strong L3 behavior: integrated evidence, correct distinction between demand and capacity, reversibility, timing, and bounded action.

The participant still does not use the shock to establish a more durable executive planning/governance architecture. The answer remains an excellent situational decision rather than a redesign of how the organization governs growth investment.

# Instrument System Audit

## 1. Historical diagnosis vs forward planning
**PASS.** The participant had to construct/challenge future assumptions and resource timing rather than merely explain prior performance.

## 2. L2 vs L3 distinction
**PASS provisionally.** The synthetic participant did more than execute supplied assumptions: they challenged and rebuilt the planning logic.

## 3. L3 vs L4 distinction
**PASS provisionally, with an important calibration finding.** Resource staging alone should not automatically produce L4. L4 planning requires evidence of material system-shaping such as durable planning standards, governance, assumption ownership, executive decision mechanisms, or comparable strategic architecture.

## 4. Revision under changing evidence
**PASS.** The Planning Shock caused a model/decision update.

## 5. Optimism stacking
**PASS.** The instrument exposed multiple individually plausible but collectively unsupported assumptions.

## 6. Target obedience
**PASS.** The participant could recommend $14.2M commitment / $15M upside without being treated as weak for disagreeing with the preliminary target.

## 7. Spreadsheet sophistication bias
**PASS provisionally.** Strong planning evidence emerged without requiring an elaborate spreadsheet.

## 8. Confidence / Insufficient Evidence
**PASS.** The run produced differentiated Confidence and did not force scores for unobserved competencies.

## 9. Clean accumulation with A1
**PASS conceptually, not empirically.** A2 provides materially stronger Forecasting & Revenue Planning evidence than A1. Overlapping GTM Systems, Analytics, and Commercial Judgment should strengthen Confidence only when evidence is distinct, not automatically raise proficiency.

## 10. Methodology defect discovery
**YES — one useful calibration issue surfaced.** The boundary between an L3 resource decision and an L4 strategic resource/governance decision needs to remain explicit. A participant can make a consequential hiring decision at L3 if the behavior is primarily situational diagnosis and response. L4 requires evidence that the participant materially shapes how consequential planning decisions are governed or made.

---

# Candidate Calibration Finding

The run strengthens the following cross-framework distinction:

> **L3 can make strong, consequential operating decisions inside an ambiguous system. L4 additionally shapes the mechanisms, standards, governance, architecture, or senior decision process through which consequential choices are repeatedly made.**

Consequential decision size alone is not sufficient evidence of L4.

This finding is consistent with the existing shared proficiency spine but should be tested in additional competencies before becoming a stronger methodology rule.

---

# Instrument Design Finding

The embedded RP-001-v0.1 dataset successfully generated planning evidence, but retaining it inside the instrument is inconsistent with the broader reusable-dataset architecture.

**Recommended next design action:** extract RP-001 into `datasets/` as a separately versioned Candidate before further A2 validation or variant creation.

The exercise should then reference the dataset rather than own it.

---

# Validation Status

This controlled synthetic run supports continued Candidate development of A2.

It does **not** establish:
- empirical inter-rater reliability;
- predictive validity;
- hiring validity;
- certification validity;
- numeric thresholds;
- a validated VP Role Profile;
- that the $14.2M recommendation is a uniquely correct answer;
- that one synthetic participant is sufficient to validate L3/L4 discrimination.

A contrasting synthetic L4 planning run should eventually test whether the instrument can positively elicit strategic planning-governance behavior rather than merely withhold L4 from a strong L3 participant.
