# Outer-Seam Calibration Test

**Status:** Candidate v0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Canonical:** No  
**Purpose:** Pressure-test the two least-developed proficiency boundaries in the current RevOps Competency Model: **L1→L2** and **L4→L5**.

## Why This Test Exists
Recent work has provided useful discrimination around L3 and L4. The five-level model remains broader than the evidence gathered so far.

The current competency model defines:
- **L1 Foundational:** understands core concepts and terminology; performs defined tasks with guidance; recognizes common patterns.
- **L2 Practitioner:** applies concepts independently in familiar situations; performs routine analysis; identifies issues; recommends reasonable next steps.
- **L4 Strategic:** shapes operating models and decisions across functions; anticipates second-order effects; manages tradeoffs; establishes standards; influences senior stakeholders.
- **L5 Enterprise / Architect:** designs or transforms RevOps systems at organizational scale; creates reusable operating frameworks; governs complexity; makes high-leverage decisions under uncertainty.

This test asks whether those distinctions can be made from observable behavior rather than title, tenure, company size, or grander language.

---

# Test A — L1 versus L2

## Shared situation
A participant receives a familiar funnel report:
- 1,000 MQLs
- 260 SQLs
- 90 Opportunities
- 24 Closed Won
- prior-quarter MQL-to-SQL conversion was 31%; current conversion is 26%
- definitions and data quality are known to be stable

The task is to identify what matters and recommend the next step.

## Performance A1 — Foundational
The participant says:

> MQL-to-SQL conversion declined from 31% to 26%, so fewer Marketing-qualified leads are becoming Sales-qualified leads. I would look into why the conversion rate dropped and compare it with previous periods.

If asked what analysis to run, the participant can calculate the decline and recognizes that source, segment, or qualification criteria may matter, but needs prompting to choose which breakdown to perform and how to interpret the result.

### Provisional judgment
**L1 Foundational**

### Why
The participant recognizes the pattern and understands the terminology, but independent application is incomplete. They can perform the work with guidance but do not yet structure and execute the routine investigation on their own.

---

## Performance A2 — Practitioner
The participant says:

> MQL-to-SQL fell five percentage points, roughly a 16% relative decline from the prior-quarter rate. Because definitions are stable, I would first decompose conversion by lead source and segment to see whether the change is concentrated. I would compare volume and conversion together so a mix shift does not masquerade as a process problem. If one source deteriorated materially, I would inspect qualification acceptance and rejection reasons there before recommending a process change.

The participant independently chooses the analysis, explains why it matters, performs the routine decomposition correctly, and proposes a reasonable next step based on the result.

### Provisional judgment
**L2 Practitioner**

### Why
The key transition is **independent routine application**. The participant does not need ambiguity, cross-functional operating-model design, or strategic resource allocation to demonstrate L2.

---

## L1→L2 calibration finding
The boundary is not “knows less” versus “knows more.” It is:

> **L1 recognizes and executes with guidance; L2 independently applies the competency in a familiar situation and produces a defensible routine next step.**

### Do not over-score L2 for
- using more terminology;
- faster arithmetic;
- requesting more data;
- knowing advanced concepts irrelevant to the task.

### Do not under-score L2 because
- the problem is straightforward;
- the participant does not propose cross-functional change;
- the participant does not display L3-style ambiguity handling.

# Test B — L4 versus L5

## Shared situation
A company has already diagnosed a cross-functional growth problem and a strong VP-level operator has:
- changed investment gates for Marketing and Sales capacity;
- introduced cohort-adjusted productivity and funnel economics into planning;
- established cross-functional executive ownership;
- created a recurring growth-investment review;
- improved decision quality in the current business.

The question is what additional observable behavior, if any, would justify **L5 Enterprise / Architect** rather than L4 Strategic.

## Performance B1 — Strategic
The participant:
- redesigns the company's GTM investment governance;
- aligns Marketing, Sales, Finance, and RevOps around shared economics;
- sets standards for when channels and capacity can scale;
- anticipates second-order effects on forecast, hiring, and pipeline;
- influences executive stakeholders;
- adapts the model as evidence changes.

The participant's work is materially strategic and shapes how this organization operates.

### Provisional judgment
**L4 Strategic**

### Why
This is excellent strategic leadership. The participant is shaping the operating model and senior decisions, but the demonstrated solution remains principally optimized for the current organization's context.

---

## Performance B2 — Enterprise / Architect
The participant does everything in B1, then goes further.

Across multiple business units, products, geographies, or portfolio companies with materially different GTM motions, the participant:
- identifies which elements of the investment-governance model are universal versus context-dependent;
- creates reusable decision principles rather than forcing one operating template everywhere;
- defines governance for exceptions, local variation, and model evolution;
- builds a common architecture that allows different motions to coexist without destroying comparability;
- establishes how evidence standards, ownership, and economic decisions should scale as complexity increases;
- deliberately manages tradeoffs between central consistency and local autonomy;
- demonstrates that the framework remains useful when assumptions, products, markets, or organizational structures change.

A strong L5 response may say:

> I would not export the current company's exact funnel thresholds across the portfolio. I would codify the decision architecture: every growth investment needs an explicit demand-yield assumption, productive-capacity assumption, economic threshold, owner, review cadence, and exception rule. Each business can set different operating values, but the decision logic remains comparable. We then govern when local variation is justified and when it represents uncontrolled drift.

### Provisional judgment
**L5 Enterprise / Architect**

### Why
The participant is no longer only shaping one operating system. They are designing **reusable architecture for governing multiple operating contexts and complexity over time**.

---

## L4→L5 calibration finding
The boundary is not “bigger company,” “more executive,” or “more transformation.” It is:

> **L4 shapes a complex operating system strategically; L5 creates reusable architecture and governance that remains coherent across materially different contexts, scale, and change.**

### L5 requires more than
- enterprise vocabulary;
- responsibility for a large company;
- multiple direct reports;
- implementing the same playbook in several places;
- making a very high-dollar decision;
- creating a framework once.

### Positive L5 evidence includes
- abstraction of durable principles from local solutions;
- explicit management of variation and exceptions;
- architecture that supports multiple GTM motions or organizational contexts;
- governance of complexity rather than elimination of all differences;
- reusable decision systems tested or reasoned across materially different environments;
- adaptation over time without loss of core integrity.

---

# Important Pressure-Test Result

The existing **Enterprise / Architect** label is directionally useful, but it creates a possible scoring trap: evaluators may confuse organizational scale with proficiency.

A participant should not earn L5 simply because they work at a Fortune 100 company or support several business units. Likewise, a participant in a smaller organization could demonstrate L5-quality architecture if the evidence genuinely shows reusable system design, complexity governance, and transfer across materially different contexts.

This suggests the eventual L5 evidence anchors should emphasize **architecture, transferability, complexity governance, and durable design principles**, not employer size.

---

# Five-Level Ruler After Outer-Seam Testing

The emerging distinctions can now be summarized provisionally:

- **L1 Foundational:** recognize and perform defined work with guidance.
- **L2 Practitioner:** independently apply the competency in familiar situations and produce defensible routine outcomes.
- **L3 Advanced:** handle ambiguity, integrate evidence, diagnose cross-functional problems, and design effective responses.
- **L4 Strategic:** shape operating models, material decisions, tradeoffs, standards, governance, and senior-stakeholder action.
- **L5 Enterprise / Architect:** create reusable architectures and governance that remain coherent across materially different contexts, complexity, scale, and change.

This is a Candidate calibration synthesis, not yet a canonical rewrite of the proficiency model.

---

# Remaining Risks

This test improves conceptual separation but does not validate the full framework. Remaining risks include:
- whether real participant responses cluster cleanly around these boundaries;
- whether two independent evaluators apply the boundaries consistently;
- whether L5 can be demonstrated fairly in a bounded assessment rather than only through work-history evidence;
- whether every competency expresses L5 in the same way;
- whether some competencies need different manifestations of enterprise/architect capability;
- whether role profiles should ever require L5 across many competencies or only selected ones.

## Design Implication
Do **not** assume every competency's L5 anchor should literally require multi-company or multi-business-unit work. The underlying construct is reusable architecture and complexity governance. Evidence may manifest differently in Data & Analytics, Systems & Automation, Change Management, Commercial Judgment, or Communication.

## Next Recommended Step
The five-level ruler now has enough conceptual definition to justify drafting a **small full-range evidence-anchor prototype for one competency**, not all eleven.

Commercial & Business Judgment is the strongest candidate because it has already received the deepest adversarial testing. A single L1-L5 competency prototype can reveal whether the full-range format is actually usable before multiplying it across the model.
