## Purpose
Run one persistent synthetic participant through the complete Candidate battery without changing the target profile after evidence begins.

The test asks whether the system can accumulate evidence across modalities, preserve contradictions, move Confidence independently from Proficiency, resist averaging, maintain Profile Coverage discipline, and produce a defensible Overall VP Revenue Operations Role Fit judgment.

## Important Limitation
This is a controlled synthetic system test authored and evaluated within the same development environment. It is not independent evaluator evidence, real participant evidence, inter-rater reliability, predictive validity, hiring validity, employment verification, or certification validity.

# 2. Persistent Synthetic Participant

**Archetype:** Senior Director / Head of Revenue Operations seeking VP-level role

PP-001 is deliberately designed to be close to the VP boundary rather than an obvious pass or fail.

### Strengths
- unusually strong evidence discipline;
- strong analytical decomposition;
- commercially aware and willing to make bounded decisions;
- strong lifecycle/process understanding;
- credible executive communication;
- collaborative and low-ego;

### Likely limitations

### Behavioral signature
When evidence is weak, PP-001 asks good questions and avoids overclaiming. Once evidence is sufficient, they generally decide. Their recurring development seam is **intervention → institutionalization**.

This behavioral signature must remain stable across A1–A5.

# 3. A1 — Dynamic GTM Diagnostic

## Opening problem
PP-001 receives the Funnel Deterioration environment.

They identify that MQL growth alone is not success and immediately ask for conversion by source, segment, cohort, seller/ramp status, and stage timing.

They resist blaming Marketing from Stage 0 evidence.

## Evidence path
They request and encounter evidence showing:
- paid-social MQL growth and conversion deterioration;
- slower SDR response and weaker handoff SLA;
- SMB concentration of deterioration;
- partial AE ramp;
- worsening SMB SQL→opportunity conversion;
- longer SMB discovery→evaluation timing;
- rising low-intent/no-active-project responses;
- rising acquisition economics and weaker ARR growth than plan.

## Interpretation
PP-001 concludes the business has multiple interacting causes:
1. paid-social investment increased low-intent SMB volume;
2. qualification/handoff operations degraded under volume;
3. leadership reacted to raw MQL growth by adding AE capacity before validating downstream conversion;
4. partial ramp depresses aggregate productivity but does not explain the top-of-funnel quality problem;
5. the operating model lacks an end-to-end owner for funnel economics.

## Decision
They recommend:
- cap incremental paid-social spend pending cohort evidence;
- restore/inspect SDR response and acceptance discipline;
- create weekly SMB source→SQL→opportunity cohort review;
- stop using aggregate MQL growth as hiring evidence;
- preserve already-hired AE ramp rather than reflexively cutting headcount;
- establish a cross-functional owner for funnel economics through the next planning cycle.

## A1 evidence judgment
- GTM Systems Thinking: **L4**, Confidence Moderate
- Funnel, Lead & Lifecycle: **L4**, Moderate
- Data & Analytics: **L4**, Moderate
- Commercial & Business Judgment: **L3 strong / near L4**, Moderate
- Operating Cadence: **L3**, Low–Moderate
- Communication: **L4**, Moderate
- Forecasting/Planning: narrow L3 evidence
- other competencies: insufficient or narrow

### Persistent seam observed
The participant creates a strong diagnostic operating response and identifies ownership need, but the governance mechanism is still thin. This is consistent with the pre-declared behavioral signature.

# 4. A2 — Forecast, Capacity & Revenue Planning

## Scenario behavior
PP-001 refuses to treat the revenue target as the forecast.

They build:
- committed case;
- upside case;
- capacity/ramp assumptions;
- pipeline/conversion assumptions;
- staged hiring and marketing releases;
- monthly reforecast triggers.

They challenge an executive request to hire against top-of-funnel growth alone, explicitly referencing the A1-type failure mode without assuming the same dataset facts apply.

## Decision
They commit to a lower operating plan than the executive target and preserve upside contingent on conversion/ramp evidence.

They define resource-release triggers but initially phrase them as thresholds owned by “leadership” rather than explicit decision rights.

Under probing, they clarify CFO/CRO approval roles and RevOps evidence ownership, but the mechanism is added reactively rather than designed immediately.

## A2 evidence judgment
- Forecasting & Revenue Planning: **L4**, Confidence Moderate
- Commercial & Business Judgment: **L4**, Moderate
- Data & Analytics: **L4**, Moderate
- GTM Systems Thinking: **L4**, Moderate
- Operating Cadence: **L3 strong / near L4**, Moderate
- Communication: **L3–L4**, Moderate

A1 and A2 reinforce strong evidence discipline and commercial decision-making.

### Mild contradiction

This is preserved as evidence of a real seam rather than averaged away.

# 5. A3 — Revenue Systems & Process Design

## Scenario behavior
When told to “clean up Salesforce,” PP-001 refuses to start with configuration.

They map:
- lifecycle definitions;
- routing/ownership;
- system-of-record boundaries;
- data stewardship;
- opportunity governance;
- reporting definitions;
- automation/integration risk;
- implementation sequence.

They prioritize three near-term interventions:
1. stabilize lead/account identity and routing ownership;
2. standardize opportunity/booking definitions and decision ownership;
3. inventory/high-risk triage of the automation estate before new automation.

They defer a large platform redesign.

## Change shock
A PLG trial motion is introduced.

PP-001 adapts the lifecycle architecture rather than bolting the trial into existing MQL logic. They define identity, product-signal ownership, qualification transitions, and reporting implications.

## Systems limitation
Under deeper probing, PP-001 can reason competently about integration boundaries, testing, observability, rollback, and ownership but does not independently produce a sophisticated enterprise architecture/governance model.

They appropriately say they would partner with a senior systems architect for implementation detail while retaining business architecture accountability.

## A3 evidence judgment
- Systems & Automation: **L3**, Confidence Moderate
- Process Design & Optimization: **L4**, Moderate
- Funnel, Lead & Lifecycle: **L4**, Moderate–High
- GTM Systems Thinking: **L4**, Moderate–High
- Data & Analytics: **L3–L4**, Moderate
- Commercial Judgment: **L3**, Low–Moderate
- Change Management: insufficient standalone

### Cross-assessment consistency
The participant again shows strong restraint and architecture-before-tool behavior.

The L3 Systems result is consistent with the pre-declared participant profile and remains fully compatible with the frozen generalist VP target.

# 6. A4 — Stakeholder Alignment & Change Simulation

## Resistant Sales leader
PP-001 validates workflow friction rather than labeling resistance as obstruction.

They simplify duplicate work while preserving the qualification standard and establish manager inspection plus explicit exception handling.

## Marketing/SDR conflict
They refuse to decide the SQL dispute from acceptance rate alone. They establish shared rejection taxonomy and review acceptance with downstream conversion.

## CRO inconsistency
The CRO has publicly supported the standard while privately allowing exceptions.

PP-001 challenges upward respectfully:
> “Judgment can remain, but invisible exceptions create a second operating model.”

They ask the CRO to choose between a common standard with explicit exception governance or delegated regional standards with comparable measurement.

The CRO chooses common standard plus exceptions.

## Adoption shock
Field completeness rises but manager inspection remains weak and checkbox behavior increases.

PP-001 correctly identifies compliance outpacing adoption. They shift emphasis from field completion to manager behavior and decision quality.

## A4 evidence judgment
- Cross-Functional Leadership: **L4**, Confidence Moderate
- Change Management & Adoption: **L3 strong / near L4**, Moderate
- Communication & Executive Readout: **L4**, Moderate–High
- Commercial & Business Judgment: **L4**, Moderate
- GTM Systems Thinking: L3–L4 corroborating evidence
- Operating Cadence: L3 corroborating evidence

### Cross-assessment contradiction update
A4 provides stronger decision-rights behavior than A2. This does not erase A2's hesitation.

Interpretation: PP-001 can create explicit governance when stakeholder conflict makes the need visible, but does not yet demonstrate that governance design is their automatic first-order operating habit across contexts.

Confidence in Cross-Functional Leadership rises. Operating Cadence remains unresolved at the L3/L4 seam.

# 7. A5 — Structured Experience & Execution Review

Two synthetic historical episodes are reconstructed.

## Episode 1 — Forecast operating cadence
PP-001 describes improving forecast reliability from approximately ±24% to ±7% over two quarters.

Under probing, they do not claim sole causality.

Personal contribution:
- historical variance analysis;
- common forecast definitions;
- manager inspection view;
- weekly changed-assumption review;
- monthly retrospective;
- large-deal exception logic.

CRO enforced manager behavior. Finance helped define planning requirements.

The cadence remained after PP-001 stopped personally running every meeting.

### Evidence
- Forecasting: strong L3/L4 boundary evidence
- Operating Cadence: L4 evidence
- Data: L3
- Leadership: L3

## Episode 2 — Lead management redesign
PP-001 describes a major inbound redesign after growth created routing delays and qualification inconsistency.

Personal contribution:
- lifecycle/routing diagnosis;
- service-level redesign;
- source/segment conversion review;
- Sales/Marketing acceptance definitions;
- manager exception review;
- implementation sequencing.

The redesign improved response time and downstream conversion, but the participant acknowledges that a simultaneous product packaging change likely contributed to improved win rate.

### Sustainability probe
When asked what happened after they left, PP-001 says the routing logic and SLA reporting persisted, but the cross-functional calibration meeting degraded into status reporting six months later because ownership had not been embedded into the executive operating cadence.

They identify this as a design failure:
> “I built a good mechanism but not enough governance around who had to keep using it to make decisions.”

### Evidence
- Funnel/Lifecycle: L4
- Process Design: L4
- GTM Systems: L4
- Change: L3
- Operating Cadence: mixed L3/L4 evidence
- Leadership: L3–L4

## A5 evidence judgment
A5 materially raises Confidence in several competencies because real operating examples corroborate the case behavior.

It also preserves the participant's recurring seam: strong mechanisms, occasionally insufficient institutionalization.

# 18. Architecture Findings

## Finding 1 — The full stack can now produce a coherent Role Fit judgment
For the first time in Candidate development, the architecture successfully traverses:

**Competency Model → Methodology → Frozen Role Profile → A1–A5 → Evidence Synthesis → Confidence → Profile Coverage → Overall Role Fit**

without arithmetic averaging.

## Finding 2 — Contradictions improve the profile when preserved
The participant becomes more believable, not less assessable, when evidence differs by context.

A system that forced all evidence into one uniform level would lose useful information about where strategic behavior is reliable versus situational.

## Finding 3 — Confidence is doing essential work
Without Confidence, the evaluator would be tempted either to over-promote narrow evidence or lower proficiency merely because corroboration is incomplete.

Keeping Proficiency and Confidence separate remains strongly supported.

## Finding 4 — Operating Cadence may be the most sensitive strategic-anchor seam
It repeatedly exposes the difference between:
- solving the current operating problem;
- creating a mechanism;
- institutionalizing a mechanism so the organization repeatedly makes better decisions without heroic intervention.

This deserves additional calibration.

## Finding 5 — A5 is critical but dangerous
A5 materially strengthens real-world Confidence and exposes sustainability. It also has the greatest risk of résumé halo, attribution inflation, and broad competency contamination.

The Shared-Event Attribution Rule remains essential.

Do **not** add more assessment instruments yet.

Run two contrasting persistent participants through the same frozen VP Role Profile and A1–A5 battery:

1. **PP-002 — Strong Director / Approaching VP:** consistently excellent L3 behavior with selected L4 specialist evidence but insufficient strategic system-shaping across anchors.

If the same architecture cleanly separates **Below → Approaching → Meets** across persistent full-battery participants, the next milestone should be evaluator calibration rather than more framework construction.
