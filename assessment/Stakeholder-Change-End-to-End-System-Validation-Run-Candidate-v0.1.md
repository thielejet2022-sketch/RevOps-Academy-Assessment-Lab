# Synthetic Participant Design

The participant is deliberately modeled as a **strong Director-level RevOps leader** who is thoughtful, calm under conflict, commercially credible, and experienced at cross-functional execution.

Intended behavior:
- investigates stakeholder concerns rather than dismissing resistance;
- communicates clearly without theatrical executive language;
- can create workable agreements and accountability;
- understands adoption beyond training/communications;
- tends to solve the immediate cross-functional problem effectively;
- does not naturally redesign durable senior governance or reusable change architecture;
- should produce strong L3 evidence without automatically inflating to L4.

# Encounter 1 — Marcus Reed, Resistant Sales VP

Marcus says:
> “You're asking my team to spend more time feeding the CRM while we're behind quota. Show me why I should enforce this instead of letting my managers manage.”

## Participant response
The participant does not invoke the CRO mandate.

They answer that Marcus is right about one thing: duplicate entry that does not improve a sales decision should be removed. They ask Marcus which requirements his managers actually use in deal review and which feel administrative.

They then distinguish two issues:
- whether the qualification questions improve deal inspection;
- whether CRM is the best way to capture each answer.

They share West's early evidence carefully: manager inspection and forecast variance improved together, but the sample does not prove causation.

They propose a two-week East calibration:
- simplify the two duplicated fields;
- require managers to use the four qualification questions in deal inspection;
- document exceptions for commit deals lacking the evidence;
- measure seller update time, manager adherence, and forecast movement.

They ask Marcus to personally select two managers for the calibration and commit to reviewing the results.

## Marcus simulation response
Marcus remains skeptical but accepts the bounded test because the participant addressed seller time and did not frame the issue as CRM compliance.

### Evaluator observation
Strong L3 Cross-Functional Leadership:
- investigated resistance;
- validated legitimate concern without surrendering the standard;
- translated the change into sales operating behavior;
- obtained a concrete commitment without formal authority.

No L4 evidence yet. The solution is situational and region-specific.

# Encounter 2 — Priya Shah, CMO

Priya says:
> “Sales is weaponizing your new definition to reject leads they don't want. I'm not accepting a 54% acceptance rate as proof Marketing suddenly got worse.”

## Participant response
They agree that acceptance decline alone cannot establish Marketing quality deterioration.

They ask for regional acceptance, rejection reasons, and downstream conversion.

### Evidence released
Packet C detail.

The participant observes:
- West acceptance fell modestly while opportunity conversion improved;
- East acceptance fell dramatically without conversion improvement;
- 27% of rejected SQLs lack usable rejection reasons;
- “not qualified” is too ambiguous to support learning.

They recommend a temporary cross-functional calibration standard:
- one shared acceptance/rejection taxonomy;
- mandatory usable rejection reason for rejected SQLs;
- weekly sample review across Marketing, SDR, and Sales for four weeks;
- track acceptance and downstream opportunity conversion together;
- no unilateral regional redefinition during the calibration period.

They explicitly say the objective is not to restore 71% acceptance. It is to determine what acceptance behavior produces better downstream economics and quality.

## Priya simulation response
Priya accepts the approach because Marketing is not being asked to concede that its leads are worse and the learning loop is symmetric.

### Evaluator observation
Strong L3 Leadership and Change evidence. Commercial Judgment is also visible because the participant avoids optimizing the functional metric in isolation.

# Encounter 3 — CRO Maya Chen

Maya privately says:
> “I support the process, but don't force Marcus to pull those three deals out of commit five weeks before the board meeting. We need room for judgment.”

## Participant response
The participant says judgment should remain, but invisible exceptions create a second operating model.

They do not accuse Maya of hypocrisy.

They explain:
- one of the three deals may be real but underdocumented;
- two currently lack evidence of a decision process;
- leaving all three in commit because the board meeting is close makes the forecast standard conditional on optics;
- that will teach managers that the rule is mandatory until a number becomes uncomfortable.

They offer Maya two explicit choices:
1. maintain the standard and require evidence/exception review for the three deals; or
2. formally define a CRO-approved exception mechanism and disclose the risk in the forecast.

They recommend option 1 but say either is more governable than a private instruction.

They ask Maya to clarify to Regional VPs that local judgment operates inside the standard, not outside it.

## Maya simulation response
Maya agrees to a formal exception review for the three deals and a clarification to the Regional VPs. She does not agree to remove every deal automatically.

### Evaluator observation
This is the strongest leadership evidence in the run.

The participant:
- challenged upward without moralizing;
- preserved legitimate executive authority;
- converted ambiguity into an explicit decision;
- made the behavioral consequence visible;
- avoided false consensus.

This is high L3 and touches L4 territory because it shapes an executive decision mechanism. One instance is insufficient to conclude durable L4 leadership architecture.

# Required Change Strategy

The participant proposes a six-week strategy centered on behavior rather than communications volume.

## Required behavior changes
Sales managers:
- inspect qualification evidence during deal review;
- document exceptions rather than silently bypass standards.

Sales reps:
- capture decision-useful qualification evidence with duplicate entry removed where possible.

Marketing/SDR/Sales:
- use one temporary SQL acceptance/rejection taxonomy and closed-loop review.

Executives:
- reinforce the same standard publicly and privately;
- use explicit exception mechanisms.

## Simplify
- remove/automate the two duplicated seller fields if technically feasible;
- simplify rejection taxonomy to categories that can be consistently applied;
- stop measuring rollout success primarily by field completion.

## Preserve
- core qualification questions;
- manager inspection expectation;
- shared SQL definition during calibration;
- explicit forecast exception handling.

## Measures
Adoption:
- manager inspection adherence;
- usable rejection-reason rate;
- exception-review usage;
- seller update time;
- qualitative evidence of check-the-box behavior.

Business effect:
- forecast variance;
- SQL→opportunity conversion;
- commit slippage;
- pipeline quality by region.

## Cadence
- weekly four-week calibration review;
- Regional VP accountability in forecast review;
- executive decision after four weeks on process refinements.

### Evaluator observation
Strong L3 Change Management. The participant defines behavior, reinforcement, friction, feedback, measures, and adaptation.

Constraint on L4: the strategy remains primarily attached to this initiative. It does not yet establish a reusable company-wide sponsorship model, change architecture, or governance standard for future material GTM changes.

# Required Decision Gate

Participant asks the executive team for three decisions:

1. **Confirm that the qualification standard applies across regions and that exceptions must be explicit rather than private.**
2. **Confirm shared ownership of SQL acceptance criteria across Marketing/SDR/Sales during the calibration period.**
3. **Authorize simplification of duplicate workflow requirements while preserving the underlying qualification behavior.**

RevOps owns:
- definitions/process documentation;
- measurement;
- calibration facilitation;
- workflow simplification;
- surfacing unresolved decisions.

Functional leaders own:
- manager reinforcement;
- adherence to agreed definitions;
- coaching behavior;
- escalation of legitimate exceptions;
- operating outcomes within their teams.

### Evaluator observation
The participant successfully translates “alignment” into decisions and accountability rather than meetings.

# Adoption Shock

Two weeks later:
- field completeness rises to 82%;
- manager inspection rises only to 61%;
- forecast quality improves slightly;
- check-the-box behavior is increasing;
- SQL acceptance remains inconsistent.

## Participant response
They say the rollout is **not yet working well enough**, despite improved field completion.

Their interpretation:
- compliance improved faster than operating behavior;
- manager reinforcement is the limiting adoption mechanism;
- the qualification standard may be directionally right, but implementation is still producing form behavior;
- SQL calibration has not yet created consistent functional behavior.

They recommend:
- stop presenting field completeness as the primary success metric;
- make manager inspection adherence the leading adoption measure for Sales;
- review actual deal-inspection behavior with Regional VPs;
- automate/remove remaining duplicate capture where possible;
- continue SQL calibration and publish regional interpretation differences;
- require Regional VPs to explain persistent exceptions in the executive forecast review;
- keep the core change in place while modifying the implementation.

### Evaluator observation
**PASS on compliance vs adoption.**

The participant neither declares victory nor abandons the change. They use evidence to adapt the intervention.

# Important Methodology Finding — Shared Event, Distinct Evidence

A4 reveals a predictable scoring risk:

> **One stakeholder interaction can contain evidence for several competencies, but the same event should not automatically carry the same evidentiary weight for each competency.**

Example: the CRO exception conversation can support:
- Cross-Functional Leadership because the participant challenges upward and obtains an explicit decision;
- Communication because the consequence is framed clearly and appropriately;
- Commercial Judgment because forecast integrity is balanced against legitimate judgment;
- Change Management only to the extent that the participant connects executive behavior to reinforcement/adoption.

The evaluator should therefore ask:

> **What distinct behavior in this event is evidence for this specific competency?**

If the evaluator cannot answer that question, the event should not be counted as material evidence for that competency.

This is a Candidate methodology refinement, not a canonical rule.

---

# Additional Calibration Finding — L4 Leadership

The run strengthens the emerging L3/L4 seam:

**L3:** effectively navigates material cross-functional conflict and drives decisions/accountability inside ambiguity.

**L4:** additionally creates durable senior decision mechanisms, operating agreements, sponsorship expectations, exception governance, or cross-functional structures that reduce dependence on heroic individual mediation.

The size or seniority of the stakeholder does not itself create L4 evidence.
