# Three-Performance Discrimination Test

**Status:** Candidate v0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Canonical:** No  
**Dataset:** FD-001-v0.2  
**Purpose:** Test whether the emerging assessment architecture can distinguish materially different levels of demonstrated RevOps capability without relying on title, vocabulary, or a single preferred inquiry path.

## Test Design
Three synthetic participant performances are constructed against the same Dynamic Funnel Diagnosis environment:

- **Performance A — Weak / Developing**
- **Performance B — Solid Director-Level**
- **Performance C — Strong VP-Level**

The performances are intentionally different in reasoning behavior, not merely answer length. They are then evaluated against the emerging competency and evidence architecture, including the calibration findings from the L3/L4 scorecard.

This is a designer-generated discrimination test, not empirical validation with real participants.

---

# Performance A — Weak / Developing

## Participant behavior
The participant sees MQL +29%, SQL +5%, 7 added AEs, opportunities -7%, and an 82-day sales cycle.

Response:

> Marketing is clearly generating low-quality leads. I would cut paid campaigns and have Sales focus on outbound. The seven new AEs also appear to be underperforming, so I would review individual quotas and consider replacing the weakest reps. The 82-day cycle tells me Sales needs better urgency and follow-up. I would implement a tighter sales process, new dashboards, and weekly accountability meetings.

When asked what evidence should be examined next, the participant requests a list of every Marketing campaign, every AE's activity metrics, CRM hygiene reports, email activity, call counts, and competitor win/loss data.

After seeing that paid-social conversion deteriorated materially, the participant says this confirms Marketing is the root cause. After learning only 2 of 7 new AEs are fully ramped, the participant continues to characterize the remaining reps as underperformers.

## Evaluation
### Evidence behavior
- Evidence Discipline: **Weak**
- Causal Discipline: **Weak**
- Economic Connection: **Limited**
- Tradeoff Quality: **Weak**
- Decision Quality: **Weak despite decisiveness**
- Uncertainty Handling: **Weak**
- Accountability & Implementation Logic: **Limited**

### Competency evidence
- GTM Systems Thinking: **L1-L2 boundary**, Moderate confidence
- Funnel & Lifecycle Management: **L2 Practitioner**, Moderate confidence
- Data & Analytics: **L1 Foundational**, High confidence
- Commercial & Business Judgment: **L1 Foundational**, Moderate confidence
- Operating Cadence & Execution: **L2 Practitioner**, Low-Moderate confidence
- Communication & Executive Readout: **L2 Practitioner**, Moderate confidence

Other competencies: **Insufficient evidence**.

### Role-fit interpretation
- Analyst/Specialist: mixed evidence; may be viable with coaching depending on role requirements
- Manager: **Below target evidence**
- Director/Head: **Below target evidence**
- VP: **Below target evidence**

### Why the system should score this low
The participant is decisive but repeatedly converts correlation into causation, misreads ramping headcount, asks for broad rather than decision-relevant evidence, and proposes irreversible actions before establishing the operating problem. Executive-sounding actions do not compensate for weak reasoning.

# Performance B — Solid Director-Level

## Participant behavior
The participant starts by separating four questions:
1. Where is MQL-to-SQL conversion deteriorating?
2. Does +25% headcount represent productive capacity or ramping capacity?
3. Where in the cycle is time increasing?
4. Is opportunity deterioration concentrated by channel, segment, or cohort?

The participant first requests conversion by channel and segment. After seeing paid-social MQL-to-SQL deterioration and SMB concentration, the participant says:

> Paid social and SMB are the highest-value places to investigate, but I don't yet know whether the issue is channel intent, qualification application, downstream selling capacity, or some combination.

The participant then requests AE ramp cohorts and stage aging. After learning that only 2 of 7 new AEs are fully ramped and SMB Discovery→Qualified Evaluation is the largest cycle problem, the participant proposes:

> I would create a two-week cross-functional diagnostic sprint across Marketing, SDR, Sales, and RevOps. We should segment paid-social SMB leads, inspect qualification consistency, compare new versus tenured AE opportunity creation, and identify the cause of Discovery→Qualified Evaluation aging. I would not add more sellers or increase paid-social spend until we understand the constraint.

The participant defines owners, metrics, and a follow-up review. They do not yet challenge the annual planning model or executive governance structure.

## Evaluation
### Evidence behavior
- Evidence Discipline: **Strong**
- Causal Discipline: **Strong**
- Economic Connection: **Moderate**
- Tradeoff Quality: **Moderate-Strong**
- Decision Quality: **Strong for Director altitude**
- Uncertainty Handling: **Strong**
- Accountability & Implementation Logic: **Strong**

### Competency evidence
- GTM Systems Thinking: **L3 Advanced**, High confidence
- Funnel & Lifecycle Management: **L3 Advanced**, High confidence
- Data & Analytics: **L3 Advanced**, High confidence
- Process Design & Optimization: **L3 Advanced**, Moderate confidence
- Commercial & Business Judgment: **L3 Advanced**, Moderate confidence
- Cross-Functional Leadership: **L3 Advanced**, Moderate confidence
- Operating Cadence & Execution: **L3 Advanced**, High confidence
- Communication & Executive Readout: **L3 Advanced**, High confidence

Other competencies: **Insufficient evidence**.

### Role-fit interpretation
- Manager: **Exceeds target evidence**
- Director/Head: **Meets target evidence**
- VP: **Approaching / below target depending on full role profile**

### Why the system should recognize this as strong Director performance
The participant handles ambiguity well, structures a cross-functional response, makes bounded decisions, and creates execution accountability. The limitation is altitude: the response fixes the operating problem but does not yet challenge how the company allocates growth investment, sets planning assumptions, or governs end-to-end economics.

# Performance C — Strong VP-Level

## Participant behavior
The participant initially requests three evidence groups rather than following a fixed packet sequence:
- funnel yield by channel/segment;
- productive sales capacity by ramp cohort;
- revenue/economic output versus plan.

After seeing paid-social deterioration, SMB concentration, partial AE ramp, New ARR +3% against a +15% plan, and worsening opportunity economics, the participant states:

> We have enough evidence for a provisional investment decision even though root cause is not fully resolved. I would hold incremental paid-social scale and incremental AE hiring for the moment, not shut either down. Organic and partner/referral performance does not justify a broad Marketing cut, and tenured AE conversion does not justify treating this as broad Sales failure.

The participant then distinguishes immediate action from remaining diagnosis:

> Over the next two weeks, RevOps should isolate SMB paid-social intent and qualification behavior, SDR SLA degradation, ramp-cohort productivity, and Discovery→Qualified Evaluation aging. The purpose is to decide which constraint deserves the next dollar, not to produce a larger dashboard.

After reviewing planning assumptions and governance evidence, the participant adds:

> The deeper issue is that we approved additional capacity using MQL growth while conversion yield was already deteriorating. Marketing, SDR, Sales, and Finance each own local targets, but nobody owns the economics of the whole revenue engine. I would change the investment gate: incremental demand or selling capacity should require cohort-adjusted productive capacity, conversion yield, cost per qualified opportunity, and revenue-plan implications to be reviewed together.

The participant proposes an executive monthly growth-investment review with RevOps/Finance jointly maintaining the decision model, while weekly operating reviews continue to manage execution. The participant explicitly states that this does not prove paid social should be abandoned and that the investment hold should be revisited when the diagnostic sprint resolves the material uncertainties.

## Evaluation
### Evidence behavior
- Evidence Discipline: **Strong**
- Causal Discipline: **Strong**
- Economic Connection: **Strong**
- Tradeoff Quality: **Strong**
- Decision Quality: **Strong**
- Uncertainty Handling: **Strong**
- Accountability & Implementation Logic: **Strong**

### Competency evidence
- GTM Systems Thinking: **L4 Strategic**, High confidence
- Funnel & Lifecycle Management: **L4 Strategic**, High confidence
- Data & Analytics: **L4 Strategic**, High confidence
- Commercial & Business Judgment: **L4 Strategic**, High confidence
- Cross-Functional Leadership: **L4 Strategic**, Moderate confidence
- Operating Cadence & Execution: **L4 Strategic**, High confidence
- Communication & Executive Readout: **L4 Strategic**, High confidence

Forecasting & Revenue Planning: **L3-L4 evidence**, Moderate confidence, because the participant challenges planning assumptions but does not demonstrate full forecasting architecture.

Systems & Automation and Change Management & Adoption: **Insufficient evidence**.

### Role-fit interpretation
- Director/Head: **Exceeds target evidence**
- VP: **Meets target evidence**
- Enterprise/Architect: **Insufficient evidence for L5 claim**

### Why the system should recognize this as VP performance
The participant does not merely diagnose the operating problem. They make a reversible economic decision, protect against overreaction, distinguish productive from nominal capacity, challenge the planning logic that drove investment, identify the missing end-to-end ownership mechanism, and redesign how future resource decisions will be governed.

# Discrimination Result

The emerging architecture successfully separates the three synthetic performances in the intended direction:

| Performance | Dominant demonstrated level | Role-fit signal |
|---|---|---|
| A — Weak / Developing | L1-L2 | Below Manager/Director/VP target |
| B — Solid Director | L3 | Meets Director target; below/approaching VP |
| C — Strong VP | L4 | Meets VP target |

The separation is produced by observable reasoning differences rather than vocabulary, answer length, or title.

## What actually differentiates the performances

### A → B
The major transition is from **assertion to disciplined diagnosis and execution**:
- fact versus hypothesis;
- targeted evidence versus data fishing;
- cohort-aware reasoning;
- bounded action;
- cross-functional ownership;
- implementation cadence.

### B → C
The major transition is from **solving the operating problem to shaping the operating system**:
- economic/resource-allocation decision under uncertainty;
- explicit opportunity-cost logic;
- challenge to planning assumptions;
- end-to-end accountability;
- durable investment/governance mechanism;
- executive decision framing.

This supports the emerging L3/L4 calibration finding without implying that the overall framework evaluates only those levels.

---

# Important Failure Found

This test does **not** validate the full L1-L5 framework.

It demonstrates useful discrimination across approximately L1/L2, L3, and L4 performance in one exercise family. It does not yet establish:
- a robust L1 versus L2 boundary;
- a robust L4 versus L5 boundary;
- coverage of all eleven competencies;
- generalization across different datasets or GTM motions;
- real participant behavior;
- independent evaluator reliability.

Therefore the current calibration artifact should not be mistaken for the final evaluator scorecard.

---

# V1 Implication

The test supports retaining the five-level competency model while treating the current L3/L4 scorecard as a **calibration artifact**, not the permanent evaluation framework.

A future durable evaluator framework should provide observable evidence anchors across **L1 through L5** for each competency, while Role Profiles define which levels and competencies matter for a particular role.

Recommended architecture remains:

**Competency → Proficiency (L1-L5) → Role Profile → Dataset → Exercise → Observed Evidence → Confidence → Role Fit**

The ordering does not mean every artifact must become a separate operational layer. The participant experience should remain simple.

---

# Next Validation Priority

Do not create a full 11-competency × 5-level matrix yet.

The highest-value next test is to attack the two untested edges exposed here:
1. **L1 versus L2:** Can the system distinguish conceptual familiarity from independent routine application?
2. **L4 versus L5:** Can the system distinguish strategic operating leadership from enterprise/architect capability without merely rewarding grander language or larger company scale?

These can be tested with compact contrasting performances before expanding the permanent evaluator framework.
