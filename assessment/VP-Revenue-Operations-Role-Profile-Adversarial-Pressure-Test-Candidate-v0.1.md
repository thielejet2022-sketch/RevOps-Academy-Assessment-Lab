# VP Revenue Operations Role Profile — Adversarial Pressure Test

**Status:** Candidate v0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Canonical:** No  
**Role Profile tested:** VP Revenue Operations Role Profile Candidate v0.1  
**Parent framework:** RevOps Competency Model Candidate v0.4  
**Purpose:** Test whether the Candidate VP Role Profile discriminates among contrasting capability patterns without averaging, title bias, specialist halo, charisma bias, L5 inflation, or rigid “L4 everywhere” logic.

## Important Limitation
This is a synthetic adversarial calibration exercise. The profiles are constructed capability patterns, not real participants. It is not empirical hiring validation, predictive validity, or inter-rater reliability evidence.

---

# Test Method

Each synthetic profile receives sufficient hypothetical evidence coverage for a Role Fit judgment unless the case explicitly tests insufficient evidence.

The evaluator applies the Candidate Role Profile qualitatively using:
1. Profile Coverage;
2. strategic anchors;
3. operating floor;
4. coherence;
5. Confidence;
6. role context.

No average proficiency score or numeric weighting is calculated.

The intended labels are not used as scoring inputs. They are used only afterward to determine whether the Role Profile behaves plausibly.

# Cross-Case Findings

## Finding 1 — “VP does not mean L4 everywhere” survives adversarial testing
The credible generalist VP can Meet with several L3 specialist competencies when the defining strategic anchors are L4 and the operating floor is sound.

This is necessary to avoid building an unrealistic universal-superhero profile.

## Finding 2 — Strategic anchors behave like role-defining capabilities, but should not yet become mechanical gates
Cases 1, 2, 5, and 7 suggest that GTM Systems Thinking, Commercial Judgment, Cross-Functional Leadership, and Operating Cadence are unusually discriminating for a generalist VP profile.

However, converting them immediately into four hard numeric gates would be premature. Additional synthetic and real calibration is needed.

## Finding 3 — A material L2 weakness in a defining core competency can be role-breaking
Case 7 shows why averaging is dangerous.

A participant cannot compensate for weak Commercial Judgment by being excellent in nine other domains when the role requires consequential business decisions.

## Finding 4 — Context modifiers are necessary and dangerous
Cases 3, 8, and 9 show that the same participant can legitimately Meet one VP role and Approach another.

This is a feature, not a defect, because Role Fit is role-specific.

But modifiers create evaluator discretion. Therefore a future methodology revision should likely require:

> **Material role-context modifiers must be defined before participant evidence is evaluated in selection/hiring use.**

This prevents the target from moving after the evaluator sees the person.

## Finding 5 — Specialist excellence does not equal generalist VP fit
Case 5 strongly validates separation between proficiency and Role Fit.

A world-class Revenue Systems architect may be L5 and still be Below target for a generalist VP Revenue Operations role while being exceptional for a specialized systems leadership profile.

## Finding 6 — Exceeds needs more definition
Case 6 exposes the least mature Role Fit label.

“Exceeds” currently means materially exceeding the role pattern in relevant breadth, depth, or transferability. That is directionally sound but still vulnerable to evaluator enthusiasm.

Before external/hiring use, Exceeds likely needs tighter behavioral guidance or may be unnecessary.

---

# Candidate Refinements Recommended

This pressure test does **not** modify the Role Profile. It recommends the following for a future Candidate revision:

1. Preserve the base strategic anchors as strong qualitative signals, not numeric gates yet.
2. Add an explicit **pre-declared context modifier rule** for hiring/selection use.
3. Clarify that a confirmed L2 weakness in a defining core competency may produce Below target even when most other competencies are strong.
4. Clarify that **Meets** is the intended successful Role Fit label; Exceeds should not become a prestige tier.
5. Consider whether Exceeds is needed at all before external use.
6. Preserve Insufficient Evidence whenever coverage is incomplete.
7. Do not create an average or weighted composite score to resolve edge cases.

# Validation Result

**VP Revenue Operations Role Profile Candidate v0.1: PASS for continued Candidate development.**

The profile successfully discriminated:
- strong Director from credible VP;
- generalist VP from systems-heavy role requirements;
- charismatic executive from evidence-backed operator;
- specialist excellence from generalist Role Fit;
- selected L5 evidence from automatic Exceeds;
- material core weakness from average compensation;
- base-role fit from context-specific fit;
- strong strategic-anchor evidence from complete profile coverage.

## Remaining weaknesses
- no independent evaluator calibration;
- no real participant evidence;
- no validated role-context taxonomy;
- Exceeds remains underdefined;
- no validated minimum evidence standard by competency;
- no empirical link to job performance.
