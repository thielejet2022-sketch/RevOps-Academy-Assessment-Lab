# RevOps Competency Model

**Status:** Candidate v0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Canonical:** No

## Purpose
Define the capabilities the RevOps Academy & Assessment Lab teaches, practices, and evaluates.

The model is intentionally role-aware without making job title a proxy for skill. A person's demonstrated proficiency may vary by competency domain.

## Design Principle: Competency x Proficiency
Each assessment result should answer two separate questions:

1. **What capability is being demonstrated?**
2. **At what level of proficiency is it being demonstrated?**

Role or seniority may define an expected proficiency profile, but actual scoring is based on demonstrated evidence.

## Candidate Competency Domains

### 1. GTM Systems Thinking
Ability to understand the revenue engine as an interconnected system across marketing, sales, customer success, finance, product, and operations.

### 2. Funnel & Lifecycle Management
Ability to define, measure, diagnose, and improve conversion across lifecycle stages, including qualification quality and stage integrity.

### 3. Forecasting & Revenue Planning
Ability to construct, challenge, interpret, and operationalize forecasts, capacity plans, pipeline coverage, and revenue targets.

### 4. Data & Analytics
Ability to work from evidence, identify data-quality limitations, frame analyses correctly, distinguish observation from inference, and communicate decision-relevant insights.

### 5. Process Design & Optimization
Ability to design scalable workflows, reduce friction, establish ownership, improve handoffs, and eliminate unnecessary complexity.

### 6. Systems & Automation
Ability to design and govern CRM, GTM tooling, integrations, automation, and system architecture appropriate to the operating environment.

### 7. Commercial & Business Judgment
Ability to connect RevOps analysis to economics, prioritization, resource allocation, tradeoffs, customer behavior, and business outcomes.

### 8. Cross-Functional Leadership
Ability to influence stakeholders, clarify accountability, manage competing incentives, facilitate decisions, and drive operating change without relying only on formal authority.

### 9. Operating Cadence & Execution
Ability to establish useful rhythms for inspection, decision-making, accountability, follow-through, and continuous improvement.

### 10. Communication & Executive Readout
Ability to explain complex operating situations clearly, separate fact from hypothesis, identify the decision required, and tailor communication to the audience.

## Candidate Proficiency Levels

### Level 1 — Foundational
Understands core concepts and terminology. Can perform defined tasks with guidance and recognize common patterns.

### Level 2 — Practitioner
Applies concepts independently in familiar situations. Can perform routine analysis, identify issues, and recommend reasonable next steps.

### Level 3 — Advanced
Handles ambiguous and cross-functional problems. Can diagnose root causes, challenge assumptions, design solutions, and connect work to business outcomes.

### Level 4 — Strategic
Shapes operating models and decisions across functions. Anticipates second-order effects, manages tradeoffs, establishes standards, and influences senior stakeholders.

### Level 5 — Enterprise / Architect
Designs or transforms RevOps systems at organizational scale. Creates reusable operating frameworks, governs complexity, and makes high-leverage decisions under uncertainty.

## Role Mapping Principle
Role expectations should be expressed as a **target proficiency profile**, not a single level.

Illustrative example only:

- Analyst: primarily Levels 1–2, with selective Level 3 capability
- Manager: primarily Levels 2–3
- Director / Head of RevOps: primarily Levels 3–4
- VP / Enterprise RevOps leader: primarily Levels 4–5

These ranges are not scoring rules and should not override demonstrated evidence.

## Assessment Principle
Assessments should score the proficiency demonstrated **within each competency domain**. The same participant may be Advanced in Forecasting, Practitioner in Systems & Automation, and Strategic in Cross-Functional Leadership.

This enables the Academy to support:
- learning-path placement
- role-readiness assessment
- hiring evaluation
- individual development planning
- progression over time
- comparison between current proficiency and target-role expectations

## Open Design Questions for v0.2
- Are the ten domains the correct set, or should any be merged or split?
- Are five proficiency levels useful, or is four sufficient?
- Should role profiles be standardized by job family or remain configurable?
- Which competencies are universal versus role-specific?
- How should evidence thresholds differ by proficiency level?
- How should technical execution and business judgment be balanced in scoring?
