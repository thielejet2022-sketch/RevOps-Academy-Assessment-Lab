# RevOps Competency Model

**Status:** Candidate v0.2  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Canonical:** No

## Purpose
Define the capabilities the RevOps Academy & Assessment Lab teaches, practices, and evaluates across different levels of professional maturity and responsibility.

The model is role-aware without making job title a proxy for skill. Demonstrated proficiency may vary materially by competency domain.

## Core Architecture: Competency × Proficiency × Role Profile
Assessment should answer three distinct questions:

1. **What capability is being demonstrated?** — Competency domain
2. **At what level is it being demonstrated?** — Proficiency level
3. **What level of capability is appropriate for the work being evaluated?** — Role profile

Role profiles establish expected competency patterns. They do not determine a participant's score. Evidence does.

## Candidate Competency Domains

### 1. GTM Systems Thinking
Ability to understand the revenue engine as an interconnected system across marketing, sales, customer success, finance, product, and operations.

### 2. Funnel & Lifecycle Management
Ability to define, measure, diagnose, and improve conversion across lifecycle stages, including qualification quality and stage integrity.

### 3. Forecasting & Revenue Planning
Ability to construct, challenge, interpret, and operationalize forecasts, capacity plans, pipeline coverage, and revenue targets.

### 4. Data & Analytics
Ability to work from evidence, identify data-quality limitations, frame analyses correctly, distinguish observation from inference, and communicate decision-relevant insights.

### 5. Process Design & Optimization
Ability to design scalable workflows, reduce friction, establish ownership, improve handoffs, and eliminate unnecessary complexity.

### 6. Systems & Automation
Ability to use, design, govern, and architect CRM, GTM tooling, integrations, automation, and system environments appropriate to the participant's level of responsibility. Higher proficiency emphasizes architecture and governance rather than merely greater configuration skill.

### 7. Commercial & Business Judgment
Ability to connect RevOps analysis to economics, prioritization, resource allocation, tradeoffs, customer behavior, and business outcomes.

### 8. Cross-Functional Leadership
Ability to influence stakeholders, clarify accountability, manage competing incentives, facilitate decisions, and drive operating alignment without relying only on formal authority.

### 9. Change Management & Adoption
Ability to translate operating decisions into sustained organizational behavior through stakeholder alignment, rollout planning, enablement, adoption measurement, reinforcement, and adjustment.

### 10. Operating Cadence & Execution
Ability to establish useful rhythms for inspection, decision-making, accountability, follow-through, and continuous improvement.

### 11. Communication & Executive Readout
Ability to explain complex operating situations clearly, separate fact from hypothesis, identify the decision required, and tailor communication to the audience.

## Candidate Proficiency Levels

### Level 1 — Foundational
Understands core concepts and terminology. Can perform defined tasks with guidance and recognize common patterns.

### Level 2 — Practitioner
Applies concepts independently in familiar situations. Can perform routine analysis, identify issues, and recommend reasonable next steps.

### Level 3 — Advanced
Handles ambiguous and cross-functional problems. Can diagnose root causes, challenge assumptions, design solutions, and connect work to business outcomes.

### Level 4 — Strategic
Shapes operating models and decisions across functions. Anticipates second-order effects, manages tradeoffs, establishes standards, and influences senior stakeholders.

### Level 5 — Enterprise / Architect
Designs or transforms RevOps systems at organizational scale. Creates reusable operating frameworks, governs complexity, and makes high-leverage decisions under uncertainty.

## Role Profile Framework
A **Role Profile** defines the target proficiency pattern appropriate to a type and altitude of RevOps work.

A Role Profile is not a title-based score. It identifies which competencies matter most, the expected proficiency range in each, and the kinds of evidence appropriate to the role.

Candidate role-profile families include:

### RevOps Analyst / Specialist
Emphasis on accurate execution, data and analytics, funnel measurement, systems fluency, process discipline, and evidence-based problem identification.

Typical target range: primarily Levels 1–2, with selective Level 3 capability.

### RevOps Manager
Emphasis on independent problem solving, process ownership, cross-functional execution, operational improvement, and translating analysis into action.

Typical target range: primarily Levels 2–3.

### Director / Head of Revenue Operations
Emphasis on ambiguous diagnosis, operating-model design, forecasting and planning, cross-functional leadership, commercial judgment, change management, and executive communication.

Typical target range: primarily Levels 3–4.

### VP of Revenue Operations
A distinct strategic leadership profile, not merely a more experienced Director.

Expected evidence should increasingly demonstrate enterprise GTM architecture, commercial and financial judgment, organizational design, executive influence, resource allocation, cross-functional governance, change leadership, strategic planning, and decision-making under uncertainty.

Technical fluency remains important, but the assessment should distinguish the ability to personally configure a system from the ability to govern the architecture, people, processes, investments, and operating decisions surrounding that system.

Typical target range: primarily Levels 4–5, while recognizing that proficiency may vary by domain.

## Future Specialized Profiles
The shared competency backbone may support specialized profiles without creating new universal proficiency levels.

Examples may eventually include:
- Marketing Operations leader
- Sales Operations leader
- GTM Systems / Revenue Systems leader
- Revenue Analytics leader
- PE Portfolio RevOps Leader / Operating Partner

These profiles should be created only when real use cases justify them.

## Assessment Design Principle
Difficulty should not be represented only by making datasets larger or calculations harder.

The same operating situation may test different levels of maturity depending on what the participant is expected to notice, question, connect, decide, and communicate.

For example, given the same funnel deterioration:
- an Analyst may be expected to identify and quantify the conversion problem;
- a Manager may be expected to diagnose likely causes and design an investigation;
- a Director may be expected to connect the issue across teams, operating processes, capacity, and forecast implications;
- a VP may be expected to evaluate strategic and economic consequences, challenge the operating model, establish executive accountability, allocate resources, and define the decision path.

This allows a common dataset to support multiple assessment altitudes without equating complexity with seniority.

## Evidence Principle
Scores must be based on demonstrated evidence within each competency domain. Role Profile expectations provide the comparison target, not the participant's presumed capability.

A participant may therefore be Strategic in Cross-Functional Leadership, Advanced in Forecasting, and Practitioner in Systems & Automation regardless of title.

## Intended Uses
The framework may support:
- learning-path placement
- role-readiness assessment
- hiring evaluation
- individual development planning
- progression over time
- comparison between current proficiency and target-role expectations
- evaluation of leadership readiness across different organizational contexts

- What minimum evidence is required before assigning a proficiency level?
