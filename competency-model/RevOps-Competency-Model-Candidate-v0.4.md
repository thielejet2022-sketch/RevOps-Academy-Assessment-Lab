# RevOps Competency Model

**Status:** Candidate v0.4  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Canonical:** No  
**Supersedes for testing:** RevOps Competency Model Candidate v0.3

## Purpose
Define the capabilities the RevOps Academy & Assessment Lab teaches, practices, and evaluates across different levels of professional maturity and responsibility.

The model is role-aware without making job title, tenure, employer size, vocabulary, or tool depth a proxy for demonstrated capability.

## Architecture Decision
JET reviewed the architecture represented in Candidate v0.3 and approved it as the stable design assumption for continued Candidate development:

**Competency × Proficiency × Role Profile**

with:
- a shared five-level proficiency spine (L1–L5);
- competency-specific evidence anchors;
- Confidence recorded separately from proficiency;
- Role Fit recorded separately from proficiency and Confidence.

This approval authorizes continued Candidate development on this architecture. It is **not** canonical promotion of this document, the eleven competency domains, a scoring standard, or a certification methodology.

Assessment should answer three separate questions:
1. **What capability is being demonstrated?** — Competency
2. **At what level is it being demonstrated?** — Proficiency
3. **What capability pattern is appropriate for the work being evaluated?** — Role Profile

Role Profiles establish expectations. They do not determine participant scores. Evidence does.

# Competency Domains

## 1. GTM Systems Thinking
Ability to understand the revenue engine as an interconnected system across Marketing, Sales, Customer Success, Finance, Product, and Operations.

## 2. Funnel, Lead & Lifecycle Management
Ability to design, define, measure, govern, diagnose, and improve the movement of demand through the revenue lifecycle, including lead capture, routing, qualification, lifecycle definitions, stage-entry and exit criteria, lead acceptance/rejection, recycling, handoffs, velocity, pipeline creation, progression, and conversion.

This competency includes the ability to analyze and improve conversion rates by relevant dimensions such as source, channel, segment, cohort, stage, seller/ramp cohort, and other business context.

Conversion rates are a primary observation mechanism for lifecycle health, not a separate competency. The same conversion evidence may also support Data & Analytics, Commercial & Business Judgment, GTM Systems Thinking, Systems & Automation, or Operating Cadence & Execution when the participant demonstrates the distinct reasoning associated with those competencies.

## 3. Forecasting & Revenue Planning
Ability to construct, challenge, interpret, and operationalize forecasts, capacity plans, pipeline coverage, productivity assumptions, and revenue targets.

## 4. Data & Analytics
Ability to work from evidence, identify data-quality limitations, frame analyses correctly, distinguish observation from inference, test hypotheses, and communicate decision-relevant insights.

## 5. Process Design & Optimization
Ability to design scalable workflows, reduce friction, establish ownership, improve handoffs, manage exceptions, and eliminate unnecessary complexity.

## 6. Systems & Automation
Ability to use, design, govern, and architect CRM, GTM tooling, integrations, automation, and system environments appropriate to the participant's level of responsibility.

Higher proficiency emphasizes architecture, governance, reliability, business fit, and complexity management rather than merely greater personal configuration skill.

## 7. Commercial & Business Judgment
Ability to connect RevOps analysis to economics, prioritization, resource allocation, tradeoffs, customer behavior, and business outcomes.

## 8. Cross-Functional Leadership
Ability to influence stakeholders, clarify accountability, manage competing incentives, facilitate decisions, and drive operating alignment without relying only on formal authority.

## 9. Change Management & Adoption
Ability to translate operating decisions into sustained organizational behavior through stakeholder alignment, rollout planning, enablement, adoption measurement, reinforcement, and adjustment.

## 10. Operating Cadence & Execution
Ability to establish useful rhythms for inspection, decision-making, accountability, follow-through, learning, and continuous improvement.

## 11. Communication & Executive Readout
Ability to explain complex operating situations clearly, separate fact from hypothesis, identify the decision required, frame tradeoffs, and tailor communication to the audience.

# Shared Proficiency Spine

The five proficiency levels describe increasing **altitude of demonstrated capability**. They are shared across competencies, but each competency requires its own observable evidence anchors.

The levels must not be scored by title, organizational size, answer length, vocabulary, or simple task difficulty.

## Level 1 — Foundational
**Recognizes core concepts and performs defined work with guidance.**

Typical evidence:
- recognizes common patterns;
- explains straightforward relationships;
- executes defined tasks correctly with guidance;
- knows when additional help or escalation is required.

L1 can include meaningful knowledge and correct execution, but independent application is not yet consistently demonstrated.

## Level 2 — Practitioner
**Independently applies the competency in familiar situations and produces defensible routine outcomes.**

Typical evidence:
- chooses and performs routine analysis or action independently;
- identifies issues and reasonable next steps;
- selects among familiar alternatives;
- understands routine upstream/downstream consequences;
- does not require another person to structure the ordinary work.

**L1→L2:** guided recognition/execution → independent familiar application.

## Level 3 — Advanced
**Handles ambiguity, integrates evidence across boundaries, and designs effective responses to cross-functional problems.**

Typical evidence:
- diagnoses ambiguous problems rather than only executing prescribed work;
- challenges assumptions and requirements;
- integrates multiple operating variables or stakeholder needs;
- recognizes second-order effects;
- designs bounded solutions with risks, ownership, and checkpoints;
- updates reasoning when evidence changes.

**L2→L3:** independent familiar application → ambiguous cross-functional diagnosis/design.

## Level 4 — Strategic
**Shapes material operating decisions, tradeoffs, standards, architecture, governance, and senior-stakeholder action.**

Typical evidence:
- makes or frames consequential decisions under uncertainty;
- weighs opportunity cost, reversibility, and second-order effects;
- changes how cross-functional decisions are made or governed;
- establishes standards or operating mechanisms;
- influences senior stakeholders around material choices;
- states what should not change when evidence does not support it.

**L3→L4:** diagnosing/designing a strong response to the operating system → materially shaping the system and how consequential decisions are made.

## Level 5 — Enterprise / Architect
**Creates reusable architecture and governance that remains coherent across materially different contexts, complexity, scale, and change.**

Typical evidence:
- abstracts durable principles from local solutions;
- distinguishes universal decision logic from context-dependent parameters;
- governs justified variation, exceptions, and local autonomy;
- designs reusable frameworks that remain useful across materially different environments;
- preserves integrity as assumptions, markets, systems, organizations, or GTM motions change;
- manages complexity rather than forcing artificial uniformity;
- creates architecture others can apply without the original designer present.

**L4→L5:** strategically shaping a complex operating system → creating transferable architecture for governing multiple materially different operating contexts and their evolution.

### Critical L5 anti-bias rule
L5 is not employer size, budget size, seniority, portfolio scope, or enterprise vocabulary.

Working across several companies or business units may create opportunities to demonstrate L5, but scale alone is not evidence of Enterprise/Architect proficiency.

The label **Enterprise / Architect** remains under observation for possible terminology bias even though the underlying construct is retained.

# Competency-Specific Evidence Principle

The shared proficiency spine defines altitude, not identical behavior.

A Level 4 Commercial & Business Judgment response may demonstrate strategic resource allocation and investment governance. A Level 4 Systems & Automation response may demonstrate architecture standards, platform/integration tradeoffs, and change governance. A Level 4 Cross-Functional Leadership response must manifest through leadership behavior appropriate to that competency.

> **Use one shared proficiency spine with competency-specific evidence anchors.**

Do not mechanically copy evidence from one competency to another.

# Evidence and Scoring Principles

## Demonstrated evidence over presumed capability
Score what the participant demonstrates. Do not infer proficiency from job title, résumé, certifications, confidence, employer, or years of experience.

## Proficiency and confidence are separate
Candidate confidence labels:
- **Low:** isolated or ambiguous evidence;
- **Moderate:** multiple consistent signals with limited breadth or repetition;
- **High:** repeated clear evidence across materially different situations.

## Insufficient evidence is not a low score
If an exercise does not meaningfully test a competency, record **Insufficient evidence** rather than forcing L1 or L2.

## No keyword scoring
Sophisticated terminology has no independent scoring value. Concepts matter only when used correctly in an evidence-to-decision chain.

## Healthy uncertainty
Recognizing uncertainty, requesting evidence that could change a decision, and avoiding unsupported irreversible action are positive signals. Healthy uncertainty becomes a limitation when sufficient evidence exists for a proportional/reversible decision and the participant remains unable to decide.

## Hypothesis revision
Changing a hypothesis when contradictory evidence appears is positive evidence.

## Unsupported certainty
Confident unsupported causation should constrain proficiency regardless of seniority or decisiveness.

## No packet-count scoring
Dynamic assessments score reasoning quality, not how many hidden evidence packets a participant discovers.

## Avoid double counting
One observation may touch several competencies. Score the distinct competency-specific reasoning demonstrated rather than automatically raising multiple scores.

# Role Profile Framework

A **Role Profile** defines the target competency pattern appropriate to a type and altitude of work. It identifies which competencies matter most, expected proficiency ranges, evidence appropriate to the role/context, and where lower or higher proficiency may still be acceptable.

Role Fit is separate from proficiency.

Candidate Role Fit labels:
- Below target
- Approaching target
- Meets target
- Exceeds target
- Insufficient evidence

## RevOps Analyst / Specialist
Emphasis on accurate execution, data/analytics, funnel and lead measurement, systems fluency, process discipline, and evidence-based problem identification.

Typical target range: primarily L1–L2, with selective L3 capability.

## RevOps Manager
Emphasis on independent problem solving, process ownership, lead/lifecycle management, cross-functional execution, operational improvement, and translating analysis into action.

Typical target range: primarily L2–L3.

## Director / Head of Revenue Operations
Emphasis on ambiguous diagnosis, operating-model design, forecasting/planning, cross-functional leadership, commercial judgment, change management, and executive communication.

Typical target range: primarily L3–L4.

## VP of Revenue Operations
A distinct strategic leadership profile, not merely a more experienced Director.

Expected evidence increasingly includes enterprise GTM architecture, commercial/financial judgment, organizational design, executive influence, resource allocation, cross-functional governance, change leadership, strategic planning, and decisions under uncertainty.

Technical fluency remains important, but personal configuration skill is not synonymous with systems leadership.

Typical target range: primarily L4–L5, while recognizing meaningful competency variation.

## Future Specialized Profiles
Create only when real use cases justify them. Potential examples include Marketing Operations leader, Sales Operations leader, GTM Systems / Revenue Systems leader, Revenue Analytics leader, and PE Portfolio RevOps Leader / Operating Partner.

Do not create new universal proficiency levels for specialized roles.

# Assessment Design Principles

## Seniority is not numerical difficulty
Higher proficiency is not represented merely by larger datasets, harder arithmetic, more fields, or more complicated software.

## Dynamic evidence
For ambiguous assessments, participants should be able to request relevant evidence, revise hypotheses, and make decisions as information emerges. Hidden evidence is a responsive environment, not an answer key.

## Decision threshold
At higher proficiency levels, observe whether the participant recognizes when enough evidence exists for a bounded decision. Perfect certainty is not required.

## L5 assessment caution
Some L5 capabilities may require multiple contrasting contexts, work-history evidence, or longitudinal evidence. Do not manufacture artificial enterprise complexity solely to force an L5 score.

# Current Validation Status

## Supported at Candidate level
- five proficiency levels remain conceptually useful;
- adjacent-level boundaries can be expressed behaviorally;
- Role Profile, proficiency, and Confidence remain separate;
- competency-specific evidence anchors are required;
- evidence quality matters more than vocabulary or title;
- healthy uncertainty and hypothesis revision can be positive evidence;
- higher proficiency increasingly involves decisions, governance, architecture, and transferability;
- L5 must not become a proxy for employer scale;
- lead management and conversion belong explicitly within Funnel, Lead & Lifecycle Management rather than requiring a twelfth competency.

## Not yet established
- empirical inter-rater reliability;
- predictive validity for hiring or job performance;
- full evidence anchors for all eleven competencies;
- whether all eleven domains remain sufficiently distinct in repeated real assessments;
- weighting by Role Profile;
- numeric scoring;
- certification thresholds;
- durability of proficiency judgments over time.

Do not represent the Candidate framework as validated certification methodology.

3. Can Communication & Executive Readout resist polished-language inflation?
4. What minimum evidence is required before assigning a proficiency level in each competency?
5. Which competencies should be emphasized differently by Role Profile?
6. Should target ranges remain descriptive or eventually become numeric?

9. Does the label `Enterprise / Architect` create enough scale bias to warrant renaming L5 while retaining its construct?
