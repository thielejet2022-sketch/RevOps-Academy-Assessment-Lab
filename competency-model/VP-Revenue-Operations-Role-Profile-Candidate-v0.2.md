## Purpose
Define the competency pattern expected of VP Revenue Operations work so Role Fit can be evaluated against explicit evidence rather than title intuition, averaging, or a hidden expectation that every competency must be L4.

## Critical Boundary
This Role Profile describes the **work**, not a person's title.

A participant does not receive VP-level proficiency because they have held a VP title. A Director, consultant, operator, or other participant may demonstrate evidence that meets this profile. A current VP may fail to demonstrate it.

This Candidate is not a hiring standard, certification threshold, compensation benchmark, or claim that all VP Revenue Operations jobs are identical.

---

# Role Thesis

A VP Revenue Operations is accountable for helping the organization operate the revenue engine as a coherent business system.

The distinguishing altitude is not greater personal execution volume or deeper configuration skill. It is the ability to shape how consequential GTM decisions are made, governed, measured, resourced, and adapted across functions.

A credible VP profile therefore requires strategic capability in several high-leverage domains while allowing meaningful variation in specialist depth.

> **VP does not mean L4 everywhere.**

The profile should look like a coherent strategic pattern, not eleven identical bars on a chart.

# Candidate Competency Expectations

The ranges below are Role Profile expectations, not participant scores and not arithmetic weights.

## 1. GTM Systems Thinking
**Expected range:** L4  
**Role criticality:** Core / Strategic Anchor

Expected VP evidence:
- sees Marketing, Sales, Customer Success, Finance, Product, systems, data, incentives, capacity, and customer motion as interacting parts of one revenue system;
- identifies system-level causes rather than optimizing functional metrics in isolation;
- shapes material cross-functional operating decisions and governance;
- recognizes second-order effects and local/global tradeoffs.

Why L4: this is central to the role's reason for existing. A consistently L3 profile here would indicate strong cross-functional diagnosis but insufficient evidence of shaping the revenue operating system.

## 2. Funnel, Lead & Lifecycle Management
**Expected range:** L3–L4  
**Role criticality:** Important

Expected VP evidence:
- can diagnose and govern end-to-end lifecycle health;
- understands conversion, velocity, routing, qualification, ownership, stage integrity, pipeline creation, and handoffs;
- can shape lifecycle standards and investment/operating decisions when the business requires it.

Why not mandatory L4: a VP may operate in an environment with strong lifecycle leadership below them while personally demonstrating sufficient L3 depth plus strategic integration through GTM Systems Thinking and Commercial Judgment.

## 3. Forecasting & Revenue Planning
**Expected range:** L3–L4  
**Role criticality:** Core

Expected VP evidence:
- challenges targets and assumptions with pipeline, capacity, ramp, productivity, conversion, timing, and economic evidence;
- distinguishes objective, commitment, forecast, scenario, and upside where appropriate;
- helps govern resource release and planning assumptions;
- updates the operating plan when evidence changes.

A strong L3 may be acceptable when the participant demonstrates L4 strategic integration elsewhere and the role context does not make RevOps the principal planning architect. Materially weak planning evidence is not compatible with this profile.

## 4. Data & Analytics
**Expected range:** L3–L4  
**Role criticality:** Core

Expected VP evidence:
- frames analysis around decisions;
- decomposes misleading aggregates;
- distinguishes observation, inference, and causation;
- understands data-quality limitations;
- creates or governs decision-useful measurement rather than dashboard volume;
- resists false precision and metric gaming.

The VP need not be the strongest analyst on the team. They must be able to govern analytical truth well enough to make consequential decisions.

## 5. Process Design & Optimization
**Expected range:** L3–L4  
**Role criticality:** Important

Expected VP evidence:
- can design or govern cross-functional processes with clear states, ownership, handoffs, exceptions, controls, and measurement;
- understands when to standardize and when variation is justified;
- avoids automating ambiguous or broken process;
- can create durable operating mechanisms when process is strategically material.

L4 is particularly relevant in transformation-heavy or first-RevOps-leader environments.

## 6. Systems & Automation
**Expected range:** L3  
**Role criticality:** Important, context-sensitive

Expected VP evidence:
- understands system-of-record boundaries, integration/automation implications, data ownership, reliability, governance, testing, change risk, and business fit;
- can make or govern architecture decisions without needing to be the deepest administrator or developer;
- distinguishes platform mechanics from operating architecture.

Why L3 rather than universal L4: some VP roles directly own Revenue Systems architecture; others lead strong technical specialists. Personal configuration depth is not the construct. A systems-heavy VP Role Profile variant may legitimately require L4.

## 7. Commercial & Business Judgment
**Expected range:** L4  
**Role criticality:** Core / Strategic Anchor

Expected VP evidence:
- connects GTM operations to revenue, margin, CAC/payback, capacity, productivity, risk, customer economics, and opportunity cost as relevant;
- makes or frames consequential resource and investment tradeoffs;
- knows when evidence supports action and when restraint is required;
- protects the business from functional optimization that destroys total-system economics.

Why L4: VP Revenue Operations is not merely a process or analytics role. Material business judgment is a defining expectation.

## 8. Cross-Functional Leadership
**Expected range:** L4  
**Role criticality:** Core / Strategic Anchor

Expected VP evidence:
- influences senior stakeholders without relying solely on authority;
- makes competing incentives and decision rights explicit;
- creates durable operating agreements;
- challenges upward appropriately;
- converts disagreement into governable decisions and accountability;
- preserves useful conflict rather than manufacturing consensus.

Why L4: the role operates through functions it often does not directly control. Strong L3 facilitation alone is insufficient for a full VP profile.

## 9. Change Management & Adoption
**Expected range:** L3–L4  
**Role criticality:** Important

Expected VP evidence:
- translates operating decisions into behavior;
- identifies friction, incentives, manager reinforcement, sponsorship, feedback, and adoption signals;
- distinguishes compliance from adoption;
- adapts implementation based on evidence;
- can govern material GTM change when the context requires it.

L4 becomes more important in transformation, integration, PE-backed, or first-RevOps-leader contexts.

## 10. Operating Cadence & Execution
**Expected range:** L4  
**Role criticality:** Core / Strategic Anchor

Expected VP evidence:
- creates decision rhythms that convert strategy into accountable action;
- distinguishes status reporting from operating inspection;
- changes cadence as the system matures;
- establishes mechanisms that persist without heroic personal intervention;
- feeds operating learning back into planning, governance, and resource decisions.

Why L4: strategy without an operating mechanism is not sufficient VP evidence.

## 11. Communication & Executive Readout
**Expected range:** L3–L4  
**Role criticality:** Core

Expected VP evidence:
- separates fact, hypothesis, decision, action, and restraint;
- frames material tradeoffs for executives;
- communicates uncertainty without becoming indecisive;
- adapts framing to stakeholder needs without changing the underlying truth;
- can make complex operating problems decision-ready.

Polish, vocabulary, presentation style, accent, charisma, or brevity alone do not establish proficiency.

# Strategic Anchors

For continued Candidate testing, the base generalist VP profile treats these four competencies as **Strategic Anchors**:
1. GTM Systems Thinking;
2. Commercial & Business Judgment;
3. Cross-Functional Leadership;
4. Operating Cadence & Execution.

The adversarial pressure test found these unusually discriminating between strong Director-level performance and VP-level system shaping.

## Guardrail
Strategic Anchors are **strong qualitative signals, not mechanical gates yet**.

Do not calculate “three of four anchors = pass” or similar formulas.

A profile lacking L4 evidence across most Strategic Anchors should not normally be described as Meets target for a generalist VP Revenue Operations role. Edge cases require explicit reasoning and role context.

# Candidate Profile Pattern

A participant who **Meets** the VP Revenue Operations Role Profile should normally demonstrate:

## Strategic system-shaping pattern
Credible L4 evidence across a meaningful set of the Strategic Anchors.

## Strong operating floor
No material core competency should be supported only by routine/familiar L2 behavior.

A confirmed L2 weakness in a defining core competency may be **role-breaking**, even when most other competencies are strong. The evaluator must explain why the weakness materially interferes with the work of the target role rather than applying an automatic gate.

A VP profile may contain L3 competencies. That is expected. The question is whether the overall pattern shows sufficient strategic system-shaping capability for the role.

## Context-appropriate specialist depth
The participant need not be L4 in every specialist competency.

Examples:
- Systems & Automation may be L3 for a generalist VP with strong architecture judgment and capable technical leadership below them;
- Funnel/Lifecycle may be L3 where dedicated leaders own detailed lifecycle operations;
- Process Design may be L3 in a mature operating environment but may need L4 in a transformation role.

## Evidence breadth
Overall Role Fit requires enough evidence across the profile to support the breadth of the claim.

Do not infer missing competencies from strengths elsewhere.

---

# Role Fit Interpretation

## Below target
Evidence demonstrates a material gap between the participant's observed capability pattern and the strategic/operating requirements of the profile.

Examples may include:
- predominantly L2 evidence in core competencies;
- strong specialist execution without cross-functional strategic judgment;
- repeated inability to make bounded decisions under ambiguity;
- a confirmed role-breaking weakness in Commercial Judgment, Leadership, GTM Systems Thinking, Operating Cadence, or another competency made defining by the role context.

## Approaching target
Evidence demonstrates strong capability and meaningful VP-relevant behaviors, but strategic system-shaping evidence is incomplete or inconsistent across the role's defining competencies.

A strong Director-level profile will often land here.

## Meets target
**Meets is the intended successful Role Fit label.**

Evidence supports a coherent VP pattern:
- strategic system-shaping capability is demonstrated across a meaningful set of defining competencies;
- remaining material competencies show sufficient operating depth;
- weaknesses do not undermine the participant's ability to perform the role as defined in context;
- Profile Coverage and Confidence are sufficient for the breadth of the claim.

**Meets does not require L4 in all eleven competencies.**

## Exceeds target — Experimental
Exceeds remains available for Candidate testing but should **not** be treated as a prestige tier.

Use only when evidence shows capability that materially exceeds the target role pattern in relevant breadth, depth, or transferability, not merely because:
- one competency is L5;
- the participant has a larger title;
- the participant has managed greater scale;
- the participant is unusually polished;
- the participant comfortably Meets the role.

If the evaluator cannot explain the practical role-relevant difference between Meets and Exceeds, use **Meets**.

Whether Exceeds should remain in the final Role Fit vocabulary is an open design question.

## Insufficient evidence
Use when evidence coverage or quality does not support the breadth of the Role Fit claim.

Do not convert missing evidence into Below target.

# Context Modifiers

The base profile should remain stable, but evidence expectations may shift with the actual role context.

Potential modifiers include:
- first RevOps leader vs mature RevOps organization;
- startup/growth-stage vs mature company;
- PLG, enterprise, SMB, channel, usage-based, services, or mixed GTM motion;
- PE-backed transformation or integration environment;
- direct ownership of Revenue Systems / BI / Deal Desk / Enablement / Marketing Ops / Sales Ops;
- international/multi-business-unit complexity;
- major CRM/data transformation;
- acquisition/integration responsibility.

Modifiers may increase the importance or expected depth of particular competencies. They should not silently redefine the shared proficiency levels.

Example:
A VP role explicitly accountable for Revenue Systems architecture may require L4 Systems & Automation. That is a **role-context requirement**, not a claim that all VP RevOps professionals must personally be L4 systems architects.

## Pre-Declared Context Modifier Rule
For hiring, selection, certification-like, or other consequential assessment use:

> **Material role-context modifiers must be defined before participant evidence is evaluated.**

The target must not move after the evaluator sees the participant's strengths or weaknesses.

If a material modifier is discovered legitimately during the assessment because the role itself was incompletely defined, document the role-definition change and do not silently rescore the participant against the new target.

In Academy/coaching use, modifiers may be explored more flexibly because the purpose is developmental rather than selection.

# Anti-Bias Guardrails

Do not treat the following as independent evidence of VP Role Fit:
- VP/Head/Director title;
- years of experience;
- company ARR or employee count;
- number of direct reports;
- board exposure;
- PE ownership;
- Salesforce/HubSpot expertise;
- sophisticated vocabulary;
- presentation polish;
- résumé metrics without attribution;
- having managed a large transformation;
- employer prestige.

These may create contexts in which capability can be demonstrated. They do not establish the capability.

---

# Relationship to L5

A VP Revenue Operations does **not** need to be L5.

L5 represents reusable architecture and governance across materially different contexts. Some VP operators may demonstrate L5 in selected competencies, but requiring L5 broadly would collapse job altitude into proficiency and recreate title/scale bias.

Likewise, a participant may demonstrate L5 capability without holding a VP title.

---

# Candidate Overall Role Fit Decision Rule

Until further validation, do **not** calculate an average proficiency score or weighted numeric total.

For an Overall VP Revenue Operations Role Fit judgment, the evaluator should establish:

1. **Coverage:** Is there sufficient evidence across the material profile?
2. **Strategic Anchors:** Is L4 system-shaping evidence present across a meaningful set of defining competencies?
3. **Operating Floor:** Are any material competencies clearly too weak for the role, including a confirmed role-breaking L2 weakness?
4. **Coherence:** Do the competencies combine into a credible operating pattern rather than disconnected specialist strengths?
5. **Confidence:** Is the evidence strong enough, given the consequence and breadth of the claim?
6. **Context:** Was the target role and any material context modifier defined before evaluation, and does the participant's evidence fit that target?

If these cannot be answered defensibly, use **Insufficient evidence** rather than manufacturing precision.

Do not create an average or weighted composite score to resolve edge cases.

# Pressure-Test Findings Incorporated in v0.2

Candidate v0.2 incorporates these tested refinements from the v0.1 adversarial pressure test:

1. Strategic Anchors are named explicitly but remain qualitative rather than numeric gates.
2. Material role-context modifiers must be pre-declared for consequential use.
3. A confirmed L2 weakness in a defining core competency may be role-breaking even when the rest of the profile is strong.
4. Meets is explicitly the intended successful Role Fit label.
5. Exceeds is marked Experimental and must not become a prestige tier.
6. Insufficient Evidence is preserved when coverage is incomplete.
7. No averaging or weighted composite score is permitted as an edge-case shortcut during Candidate testing.
