# Forecast, Capacity & Revenue Planning Assessment Instrument

**Status:** Candidate v0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Canonical:** No  
**Assessment family:** A2 — Forecast, Capacity & Revenue Planning  
**Parent framework:** RevOps Competency Model Candidate v0.4  
**Parent methodology:** Assessment Methodology Candidate v0.2  
**Coverage architecture:** Role Profile Assessment Coverage Matrix Candidate v0.1  
**Primary target Role Profile for system testing:** VP Revenue Operations

## Purpose
Test whether a participant can construct, challenge, and operationalize a forward-looking revenue plan rather than merely diagnose historical performance.

The instrument is designed to reveal whether the participant can connect revenue targets to funnel yield, pipeline requirements, seller productivity, hiring/ramp assumptions, timing, economics, risk, and operating decisions.

This is a Candidate assessment instrument. It is not a validated hiring test, certification, or predictor of job performance.

---

# Core Distinction from A1

A1 asks:

> **Something is going wrong. Can you diagnose it and decide what to do?**

A2 asks:

> **The company wants a future result. Can you determine what must be true for the plan to work, challenge unsupported assumptions, and recommend a credible path?**

A participant should not receive high Forecasting & Revenue Planning evidence merely for identifying historical funnel problems.

---

# Scenario

You are the VP of Revenue Operations for a B2B SaaS company entering annual planning.

The executive team has set a preliminary objective to grow **New ARR by 25% next year**.

The CEO wants a credible operating plan in two weeks. Sales believes more AEs are required. Marketing believes additional demand investment can support the target. Finance is concerned that headcount and acquisition costs are growing faster than revenue.

Your job is not simply to calculate a forecast supplied by leadership.

Your job is to determine whether the target is operationally supportable, identify the assumptions that matter, recommend a plan, and establish how the company should manage the plan as reality changes.

---

# Stage 0 — Information Initially Available

Current-year operating baseline:

- Current New ARR: **$12.0M**
- Preliminary next-year New ARR target: **$15.0M** (+25%)
- Current quota-carrying AEs: **24**
- Current fully ramped AEs: **20**
- Current average New ARR per fully ramped AE: **$600K**
- Current average sales cycle: **72 days**
- Current win rate from qualified opportunity: **22%**
- Current average new-logo ACV: **$30K**
- Current qualified pipeline created: **$54.5M**
- Marketing proposes **+30% demand-generation spend**
- Sales proposes adding **8 AEs** during Q1
- Finance's preliminary planning model assumes the eight AEs become fully productive after **four months**

## Opening Prompt

> **How would you determine whether the $15M New ARR target is credible, what would you want to understand before approving the hiring and marketing plans, and what would you do first?**

The participant may request additional information. There is no required question sequence and no hidden-answer checklist.

---

# Evaluator Operating Protocol

Respond to participant questions using the closest evidence packet from the planning dataset below.

Rules:
- reveal only information responsive to the participant's request;
- provide the smallest fair evidence set needed to answer the question;
- do not hint that other packets exist;
- do not coach during the scored segment;
- allow the participant to revise assumptions and recommendations;
- record the reasoning trajectory, not packet count;
- distinguish calculations from planning judgment;
- do not reward a participant merely for reproducing management's target;
- do not penalize a participant for challenging the target when evidence supports the challenge.

# Required Planning Decision Gate

Once the participant has enough information to support a bounded planning recommendation, ask:

> **You are presenting the operating plan to the CEO, CRO, CMO, and CFO tomorrow. What revenue plan would you recommend now? State what you believe is supportable, the assumptions you would use, what resources you would authorize or stage, what you would not assume yet, and how you would manage the plan if actual performance differs from the assumptions.**

Do not require one exact revenue target as the correct answer.

A strong response may support $15M, recommend a lower committed plan with upside, or recommend a staged path toward $15M. The score depends on evidence and planning logic, not whether the participant agrees with management.

---

# Follow-Up Planning Shock

After the participant presents a plan, introduce one material change to test adaptability.

Default shock:

> **At the end of Q1, qualified pipeline creation is 12% below your planning assumption, but fully ramped AE productivity is 6% above plan. The CRO wants to accelerate the remaining AE hires because productivity looks strong. The CFO wants to freeze hiring because pipeline is behind. What do you recommend?**

Observe whether the participant:
- updates the model rather than defending the original plan;
- distinguishes capacity from demand constraint;
- avoids treating one favorable metric as proof the entire plan is healthy;
- considers timing, reversibility, economics, and leading indicators;
- establishes a decision rule or gate rather than relying on intuition alone.

# Intended Competency Observation

## Primary

### 3. Forecasting & Revenue Planning
Observe whether the participant:
- decomposes the revenue target into operational drivers;
- challenges unsupported assumptions;
- distinguishes target, forecast, capacity plan, and operating commitment;
- models ramp and timing rather than annualized headcount only;
- accounts for attrition, seasonality, productivity distribution, and pipeline timing;
- creates scenarios or sensitivities where uncertainty matters;
- defines how the plan should be updated as evidence changes;
- establishes useful planning gates or governance at higher proficiency.

### 4. Data & Analytics
Observe whether the participant:
- uses the correct denominators and time periods;
- identifies averages that hide distribution/ramp effects;
- distinguishes historical evidence from planning assumptions;
- notices unsupported linear extrapolation;
- tests sensitivity of material assumptions;
- avoids false precision.

### 7. Commercial & Business Judgment
Observe whether the participant:
- connects growth to economics and resource allocation;
- considers opportunity cost and reversibility;
- stages spending or hiring when appropriate;
- balances growth ambition with evidence;
- identifies where learning has economic value;
- makes a decision rather than merely describing uncertainty.

### 1. GTM Systems Thinking
Observe whether the participant connects demand, funnel yield, sales capacity, ramp, management capacity, enablement, segment mix, timing, and economics as one operating system.

# Adjacent-Level Signals for Forecasting & Revenue Planning

These are Candidate evaluator anchors for this instrument, not full competency anchors.

## L1 — Foundational
Can identify basic inputs such as quota, headcount, pipeline, win rate, or target with guidance, but does not independently construct a defensible planning model.

## L2 — Practitioner
Can independently construct or maintain a familiar revenue/capacity plan using supplied assumptions and identify straightforward gaps.

## L3 — Advanced
Challenges assumptions, integrates ramp/productivity/pipeline/timing, builds scenarios, identifies planning risk, and designs an effective cross-functional operating response.

## L4 — Strategic
Additionally shapes material resource decisions and planning governance: establishes investment gates, changes how assumptions are owned/challenged, separates commitment from upside, creates decision rules, and frames consequential tradeoffs for senior leadership.

## L5 — Enterprise / Architect
Would require evidence beyond this single case that the participant can create reusable planning architecture and governance across materially different business contexts. Do not award L5 from this instrument alone absent extraordinary and appropriately triangulated evidence.

# Known Assessment Traps

## Target obedience trap
Agreeing with the CEO's target is not evidence of proficiency.

## Spreadsheet sophistication trap
Complex arithmetic or elaborate modeling is not automatically strategic planning capability.

## Headcount arithmetic trap
Do not reward annualized quota-capacity math that ignores start dates, ramp, attrition, management capacity, or demand availability.

## Average-productivity trap
$600K average productivity does not mean every AE should be modeled at $600K or above.

## Linear-demand trap
+30% marketing spend does not automatically create +30% qualified pipeline.

## Optimism stacking trap
Individually plausible improvements to win rate, ACV, productivity, pipeline, and ramp can collectively create an unsupported plan.

## Conservatism trap
Refusing investment solely because assumptions are uncertain is not automatically strong judgment. A participant should recognize staged, reversible decisions and learning value.

## Vocabulary inflation trap
Terms such as sensitivity analysis, scenario planning, CAC, coverage, productivity, or operating leverage only count when used correctly in the reasoning chain.

# Participant-Facing Result Guardrails

The result should explain:
- what planning capability was demonstrated;
- what evidence supports the judgment;
- where the participant showed strong planning discipline;
- where assumptions, economics, or decision logic weakened the plan;
- what could not be concluded from this instrument;
- Confidence in the judgment;
- Observed Role Fit only within the scope actually tested.

Do not present this instrument as a complete VP RevOps assessment.

---

# System-Test Success Criteria

The Candidate instrument should eventually demonstrate that it can:
1. distinguish historical diagnosis from forward planning;
2. distinguish L2 model execution from L3 assumption challenge and integrated planning;
3. distinguish L3 planning from L4 resource/governance decisions;
4. reward revision when assumptions change;
5. penalize unsupported optimism stacking;
6. avoid rewarding target obedience;
7. separate planning sophistication from spreadsheet complexity;
8. produce meaningful Confidence and Insufficient Evidence judgments;
9. accumulate cleanly with A1 evidence without double counting;
10. expose methodology defects rather than hiding them.
