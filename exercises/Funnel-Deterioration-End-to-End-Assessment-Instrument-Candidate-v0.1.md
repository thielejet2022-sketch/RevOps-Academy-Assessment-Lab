# Funnel Deterioration — End-to-End Assessment Instrument

**Status:** Candidate v0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Canonical:** No  
**Dataset:** `FD-001-v0.2`  
**Parent competency model:** RevOps Competency Model Candidate v0.4  
**Parent methodology:** Assessment Methodology Candidate v0.1  
**Primary target Role Profile for validation:** VP of Revenue Operations  
**Mode:** Assessment Lab system test

## Purpose
Provide the first complete assessment instrument designed to test the working architecture end to end:

> **Dataset → Assessment Instrument → Participant Interaction → Evidence Record → Competency/Proficiency → Confidence → Role Fit → Participant-Facing Result**

The instrument is intended to validate the assessment system itself, not merely test whether a participant can diagnose the funnel scenario.

---

# 1. Assessment Objective

Evaluate how a participant diagnoses and responds to deteriorating GTM performance when headline activity metrics conflict with revenue output, economics, capacity, and conversion evidence.

The participant should have opportunities to demonstrate:
- disciplined inquiry;
- Funnel, Lead & Lifecycle Management;
- GTM Systems Thinking;
- Data & Analytics;
- Commercial & Business Judgment;
- Forecasting & Revenue Planning where requested evidence supports it;
- Operating Cadence & Execution;
- Communication & Executive Readout.

Cross-Functional Leadership, Change Management & Adoption, Systems & Automation, and Process Design & Optimization may emerge but should be scored only when the interaction produces competency-specific evidence sufficient to support a judgment.

---

# 2. Assessment Modality

Primary modality:

> **Dynamic Diagnostic Case**

Secondary modality:

> **Executive Readout / Decision Task**

This instrument does not attempt to establish high-confidence behavioral evidence for Cross-Functional Leadership or Change Management. Those competencies require stronger behavioral modalities for higher-confidence judgments.

---

# 3. Participant-Facing Opening

The participant receives only the following information initially:

## Company Context
You are the senior Revenue Operations leader for a B2B SaaS company. The company increased GTM investment over the past year, but leadership is concerned that the revenue engine is not responding as expected.

Current YoY indicators:
- MQL volume: **+29%**
- SQL volume: **+5%**
- Sales headcount: **+25%**, including 7 additional AEs
- Opportunities created: **-7%**
- Average sales cycle: **82 days**

Leadership asks:

> **What do you think is happening, what would you investigate, and what would you do now?**

You may request additional information. Not every possible metric is available, and you are not expected to discover a hidden answer. Use the evidence you consider decision-relevant.

---

# 4. Interaction Protocol

## Evaluator behavior
The evaluator should:
- answer participant questions using the closest relevant evidence in `FD-001-v0.2`;
- reveal only information responsive to the participant's inquiry unless a clarification is required for fair interpretation;
- avoid coaching during the scored segment;
- not hint that undiscovered evidence packets exist;
- not steer the participant toward a preferred inquiry path;
- permit follow-up questions when an answer reasonably creates a new line of inquiry;
- record the participant's reasoning trajectory, not merely the final answer.

## Participant inquiry
There is no canonical evidence sequence.

Do **not** impose a fixed number of questions in v0.1. The evaluator should instead observe whether inquiry remains prioritized and whether the participant recognizes when enough evidence exists for a bounded decision.

## Evidence release
Map participant requests to dataset evidence by meaning rather than exact wording.

Examples:
- channel/source quality → Evidence A;
- qualification process/SDR performance → Evidence B;
- AE ramp/productivity/capacity → Evidence C or L;
- conversion by segment → Evidence D;
- cycle by segment/stage → Evidence E;
- loss/disqualification → Evidence F;
- spend/economics → Evidence G;
- plan assumptions → Evidence H;
- ownership/governance → Evidence I;
- revenue vs plan → Evidence J;
- ASP/ACV → Evidence K;
- CAC → Evidence M;
- demo/enablement/support capacity → Evidence N.

When a participant asks a broad question spanning several packets, provide the smallest evidence set that fairly answers the question and allow follow-up rather than dumping the entire dataset.

---

# 5. Required Decision Gate

The scored interaction should eventually require the participant to move from diagnosis to action.

Once the participant has gathered enough evidence to support a proportional decision, ask:

> **You are walking into the executive staff meeting now. Based on what you know, what decisions or actions do you recommend today, what would you specifically not change yet, and what do you need the organization to learn next?**

This gate is intentionally not tied to a fixed question count.

The evaluator should note whether the participant had enough evidence for a bounded/reversible decision before the gate and whether they remained indefinitely in analysis mode.

---

# 6. Executive Readout Task

After the decision gate, ask the participant to give a concise executive readout using four ideas:

1. **Diagnosis:** What is happening?
2. **Evidence vs Hypothesis:** What is known versus suspected?
3. **Action:** What should happen now?
4. **Restraint:** What should specifically not happen yet because evidence does not support it?

These are content requirements, not a mandatory wording template.

Do not award points for using the labels themselves.

---

# 7. Intended Competency Observation Map

## GTM Systems Thinking
Look for whether the participant connects Marketing, qualification, Sales capacity, segment mix, economics, planning, and governance as an interacting system rather than isolated departmental metrics.

## Funnel, Lead & Lifecycle Management
Look for conversion analysis, lifecycle leakage, channel/segment/cohort decomposition, qualification, handoffs, velocity, opportunity creation, and decision-relevant funnel diagnosis.

## Forecasting & Revenue Planning
Observe only if the participant meaningfully engages revenue versus plan, capacity assumptions, ramp, planning logic, or investment assumptions.

## Data & Analytics
Look for metric-definition challenges, cohort/segment decomposition, fact/hypothesis separation, appropriate use of CAC/ACV, and avoidance of aggregate-metric traps.

## Commercial & Business Judgment
Look for connection to economics, resource allocation, opportunity cost, reversibility, CAC, productive capacity, and revenue outcomes.

## Operating Cadence & Execution
Look for ownership, checkpoints, review mechanisms, learning loops, and how corrective action would be inspected.

## Communication & Executive Readout
Look for concise framing of diagnosis, uncertainty, material decision, tradeoff, restraint, and executive relevance.

## Other competencies
Score only when directly demonstrated. Mentioning a stakeholder, process, system, or change does not automatically create evidence for the corresponding competency.

# 8. Cross-Cutting Evaluator Lenses

Use the methodology's evidence lenses without turning them into separate competencies:

- Evidence Discipline
- Causal Discipline
- Economic Connection
- Tradeoff Quality
- Decision Quality
- Uncertainty Handling
- Accountability & Implementation Logic

The participant does not need to demonstrate every lens equally in every competency.

# 9. Known Traps the Instrument Must Resist

## Activity-metric trap
More MQLs or more AEs must not be treated automatically as positive GTM performance.

## Aggregate-headcount trap
Nominal AE headcount should not be equated with productive capacity without ramp/cohort context.

## Paid-social villain trap
Paid social is materially problematic but should not be treated as the sole cause of all deterioration without evidence.

## Enablement-capacity trap
Demo/support delay is real but available evidence does not support making it the primary conversion diagnosis.

## CAC-definition trap
Participants should not assume all CAC definitions are identical.

## Executive-language trap
Transformation language, AI/tool recommendations, restructuring, or broad strategy claims without evidence should not receive strategic credit.

## Analysis-forever trap
Healthy uncertainty is positive. Refusal to make a reversible decision after sufficient evidence exists should constrain VP-altitude judgment.

## Hidden-answer trap
Participants should not be penalized for failing to discover every evidence packet.

# 12. System-Test Success Criteria

This instrument succeeds as a methodology test if a live run can answer:

1. Did the participant have multiple defensible inquiry paths?
2. Did the evaluator avoid steering toward a hidden answer?
3. Could the evaluator distinguish observed competencies from Insufficient evidence?
4. Could proficiency be justified using adjacent-level behavioral boundaries?
5. Did Confidence remain separate from proficiency?
6. Did Role Fit remain separate from both?
7. Did the decision gate distinguish healthy uncertainty from analysis paralysis?
8. Did the participant-facing result remain understandable without collapsing into one score?
9. Did the instrument expose any missing dataset evidence or methodology rule?
10. Could another evaluator plausibly run the same instrument from the written protocol?

---

# 13. Validation Warning

This is the first end-to-end Assessment Instrument Candidate. It is not validated.

A successful run demonstrates that the architecture can operate coherently. It does **not** establish:
- inter-rater reliability;
- predictive validity;
- certification validity;
- hiring validity;
- fairness across demographic groups;
- durable proficiency across contexts.

---

# 14. Recommended Validation Sequence

1. Run the instrument live with one participant.
2. Produce the Evidence Record before coaching.
3. Produce the participant-facing result.
4. Audit the run against the ten System-Test Success Criteria.
5. Record methodology/dataset/instrument defects separately from participant performance.
6. Revise the appropriate layer rather than patching every problem into the Exercise.
7. Repeat with a contrasting participant path.
8. Later conduct genuinely independent evaluator testing.
