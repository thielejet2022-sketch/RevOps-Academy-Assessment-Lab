# Funnel Deterioration — Multilevel Validation Scenario

**Status:** Candidate v0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Canonical:** No  
**Purpose:** Pressure-test RevOps Competency Model Candidate v0.2 across multiple role profiles using one shared operating scenario.

## Scenario
A B2B SaaS company reports the following year-over-year changes:

- MQL volume: +29%
- SQL volume: +5%
- Sales headcount: +25% (7 additional AEs)
- Opportunities created: -7%
- Average sales cycle: increased to 82 days

Leadership has increased marketing activity and sales capacity, but qualified pipeline creation has not kept pace.

The participant receives only these facts initially. Additional data should be requested rather than assumed.

## Universal Evidence Rules
Strong performance at every level should:

- distinguish observed facts from hypotheses;
- avoid claiming causation from the limited facts;
- identify material missing information;
- prioritize investigation rather than request every imaginable metric;
- connect analysis to an appropriate decision or action.

Seniority should be reflected primarily in scope, judgment, integration, decision quality, and accountability, not verbosity or jargon.

## Analyst / Specialist Profile

### Expected task
Identify and quantify the most important funnel signals and propose the next analysis.

### Evidence expected
A strong Analyst should notice that:

- MQL growth substantially exceeds SQL growth, suggesting deterioration in MQL-to-SQL conversion or a change in qualification/mix;
- SQL growth does not translate into opportunity growth;
- increased AE capacity has not produced increased opportunity creation;
- the longer sales cycle may create downstream revenue risk but cannot yet be attributed to a specific cause.

The participant should request focused segmentation such as source/channel, MQL-to-SQL conversion, SQL-to-opportunity conversion, cohort/time period, AE ramp/tenure, opportunity stage aging, and loss/disqualification reasons.

### Primary competencies observed
- Funnel & Lifecycle Management
- Data & Analytics
- Systems & Automation, where data retrieval or instrumentation is discussed
- Communication & Executive Readout at an appropriate level

### Failure signals
- treating +29% MQL growth as inherently positive;
- asserting lead quality is the cause without evidence;
- ignoring the SQL-to-opportunity decline;
- producing calculations without identifying what decision they inform.

## Manager Profile

### Expected task
Diagnose the operating problem, structure the investigation, and define near-term ownership and action.

### Evidence expected
In addition to Analyst-level evidence, a strong Manager should:

- organize hypotheses across marketing quality/mix, qualification definitions, SDR execution, AE ramp/productivity, routing/handoffs, process changes, and market/customer factors;
- prioritize hypotheses by likely impact and available evidence;
- define who should investigate each issue and what operating cadence should inspect progress;
- identify whether the problem is concentrated by channel, segment, team, cohort, product, geography, or tenure;
- propose practical corrective experiments without prematurely redesigning the entire GTM model.

### Primary competencies observed
- Funnel & Lifecycle Management
- Data & Analytics
- Process Design & Optimization
- Cross-Functional Leadership
- Operating Cadence & Execution

### Failure signals
- a long unprioritized list of possible causes;
- analysis with no owner or follow-through mechanism;
- assuming the issue belongs exclusively to Marketing or Sales;
- proposing major process change before isolating the failure point.

## Director / Head of RevOps Profile

### Expected task
Connect the deterioration across the revenue system, determine business implications, and establish a cross-functional correction plan.

### Evidence expected
In addition to Manager-level evidence, a strong Director should:

- examine whether demand generation, qualification, capacity planning, AE ramp assumptions, pipeline definitions, and forecast methodology are mutually consistent;
- connect opportunity decline and cycle elongation to pipeline coverage, productivity, forecast risk, CAC efficiency, and revenue-plan implications;
- challenge whether adding seven AEs was supported by sufficient qualified demand and realistic ramp assumptions;
- identify incentives or process design that may be creating poor qualification, weak handoffs, or stage inflation;
- establish a cross-functional operating mechanism with decision rights, owners, measures, and checkpoints;
- communicate what is known, what is hypothesized, what must be decided, and when leadership should expect evidence.

### Primary competencies observed
- GTM Systems Thinking
- Forecasting & Revenue Planning
- Commercial & Business Judgment
- Cross-Functional Leadership
- Change Management & Adoption
- Operating Cadence & Execution
- Communication & Executive Readout

### Failure signals
- remaining at dashboard diagnosis without connecting to the operating model;
- treating headcount as productive capacity without considering ramp and demand;
- recommending more leads or more sellers without unit-economic or funnel evidence;
- presenting conclusions to executives without confidence levels or decision framing.

## VP of Revenue Operations Profile

### Expected task
Determine whether the metrics expose a localized execution problem or a strategic flaw in the revenue operating model, and frame the executive decisions required.

### Evidence expected
In addition to Director-level evidence, a strong VP should:

- test whether the company's growth model coherently links demand investment, qualification standards, sales capacity, productivity assumptions, pipeline requirements, conversion, cycle time, and revenue targets;
- quantify or frame the economic consequences of continuing the current model, including underutilized sales capacity, acquisition efficiency, forecast exposure, and opportunity cost;
- challenge the allocation of incremental investment between demand creation, qualification, sales capacity, enablement, systems/process, and other interventions;
- distinguish a temporary ramp or channel issue from a structural GTM problem;
- evaluate whether leadership incentives, organizational ownership, definitions, or governance are producing the observed behavior;
- identify which executive decisions can be made now, which require more evidence, and what evidence threshold is sufficient;
- define executive accountability and the mechanism for sustained correction;
- communicate the issue in business terms rather than RevOps terminology alone.

### Primary competencies observed
- GTM Systems Thinking
- Forecasting & Revenue Planning
- Commercial & Business Judgment
- Cross-Functional Leadership
- Change Management & Adoption
- Operating Cadence & Execution
- Communication & Executive Readout
- Systems & Automation where architecture or instrumentation is material to the diagnosis

### Failure signals
- giving a polished operational answer without questioning the growth model;
- treating RevOps as a reporting/service function rather than an operating partner;
- prescribing a transformation before establishing whether the problem is structural;
- using strategic language without connecting it to evidence, economics, decisions, ownership, or measurable outcomes.

## Validation Findings

### What this scenario supports
The same dataset can plausibly test multiple professional altitudes without changing the underlying facts. The distinction emerges from the expected scope of reasoning and decision responsibility.

The exercise also supports keeping five proficiency levels because seniority is not represented simply by adding more technical difficulty.

### Potential model weaknesses exposed

1. **Competency overlap is real.** GTM Systems Thinking, Commercial & Business Judgment, and Forecasting & Revenue Planning may all appear in one senior-level response. Rubrics will need observable evidence definitions to prevent double-counting.
2. **Role Profile and proficiency are not identical.** A VP-targeted exercise can still reveal Level 2 or Level 3 performance in a particular domain.
3. **Context changes expectations.** Company stage, GTM motion, ACV, sales model, product complexity, and PE/growth context could materially change what strong leadership should do.
4. **Executive-language inflation is a scoring risk.** Senior candidates may sound strategic without producing evidence of strategic reasoning. Rubrics must reward explicit connections among facts, hypotheses, economics, decisions, and accountability.
5. **Hidden evidence may be necessary.** Later assessment versions may need controlled follow-up information so participants are rewarded for asking the right questions rather than inventing missing facts.

## Recommended Next Design Work
Before promoting the competency model, define observable evidence anchors for at least one competency across Levels 1–5. **Commercial & Business Judgment** is a strong first candidate because this scenario exposes meaningful differences across role altitude.

## Governance
This is a validation Candidate, not a canonical exercise or scoring rubric. It may inform revisions to the competency model but does not change canonical methodology without JET's explicit approval of a specific artifact.
