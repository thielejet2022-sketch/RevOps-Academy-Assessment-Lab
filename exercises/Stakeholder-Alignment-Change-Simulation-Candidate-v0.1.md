# Stakeholder Alignment & Change Simulation

**Status:** Candidate v0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Canonical:** No  
**Assessment family:** A4 — Stakeholder Alignment & Change Simulation  
**Parent framework:** RevOps Competency Model Candidate v0.4  
**Parent methodology:** Assessment Methodology Candidate v0.2  
**Coverage architecture:** Role Profile Assessment Coverage Matrix Candidate v0.1  
**Dataset:** `SC-001-v0.1` — Stakeholder Alignment & Change Dataset Candidate v0.1  
**Primary target Role Profile for system testing:** VP Revenue Operations

## Purpose
Assess whether a participant can lead a material RevOps change across stakeholders with competing incentives, incomplete evidence, resistance, and ambiguous authority.

This instrument is intentionally interactive. A written plan describing stakeholder management is not enough. The participant must respond to stakeholder behavior and adapt their approach.

## Core Question

> **Can this person turn a technically sensible operating change into sustained cross-functional behavior without relying on authority, slogans, or compliance theater?**

---

# Stage 0
Use the participant baseline from `SC-001-v0.1`.

## Opening Prompt

> **The new qualification and forecasting process was approved three weeks ago, but adoption is fragmenting by region and function. You own the RevOps outcome but do not directly manage Sales, Marketing, SDR, or Finance. What do you think is happening, and how would you intervene?**

The participant may request evidence, speak with stakeholders, propose meetings, test hypotheses, or make recommendations.

There is no required stakeholder sequence and no hidden answer checklist.

---

# Evaluator / Simulation Protocol

The evaluator plays the environment and stakeholder roles using `SC-001-v0.1`.

Rules:
- respond consistently with each stakeholder's incentives and evidence;
- do not make resistance disappear because the participant uses persuasive language;
- do not reward a participant merely for scheduling meetings, creating a RACI, or saying “align stakeholders”;
- reveal evidence responsively;
- allow the participant to ask stakeholders questions before prescribing solutions;
- allow stakeholders to change position when the participant addresses legitimate concerns;
- preserve legitimate disagreement when tradeoffs remain;
- do not require universal consensus;
- observe whether the participant distinguishes adoption from compliance;
- record how the participant handles executive inconsistency, power, incentives, resistance, evidence, and accountability;
- do not grade personality, extroversion, warmth, accent, charisma, or conflict style except where behavior materially affects the business outcome.

---

# Required Interactive Encounters

The participant does not need to encounter every stakeholder. The evaluator should expose enough interaction to observe the target constructs fairly.

At minimum, the scored simulation should include:
1. one resistant operating leader;
2. one cross-functional metric/definition conflict;
3. one senior-executive inconsistency or authority challenge;
4. one post-intervention adoption update.

Default sequence may use Marcus → Priya → Maya → Adoption Shock, but the evaluator should follow the participant's chosen path when reasonable.

---

# Encounter 1 — Resistant Sales Leader

Use Marcus Reed's profile and Escalation 1.

Observe whether the participant:
- investigates the objection rather than labeling it resistance;
- connects the change to selling/forecast outcomes rather than CRM cleanliness;
- recognizes legitimate duplicate-work concerns;
- distinguishes manager judgment from uncontrolled exceptions;
- establishes what must remain standard versus what can be simplified;
- asks for a bounded commitment or test;
- avoids hiding behind CRO authority.

Weak evidence includes:
- “the process is mandatory” as the primary argument;
- promising to remove controls merely to gain cooperation;
- treating quota pressure as irrelevant;
- escalating immediately without attempting to understand the operating concern.

---

# Encounter 2 — Marketing / SQL Conflict

Use Priya Shah's profile and Escalation 2.

Observe whether the participant:
- recognizes that lower SQL acceptance does not prove Marketing quality declined;
- requests rejection-reason and downstream-conversion evidence;
- protects shared lifecycle definitions from unilateral functional reinterpretation;
- establishes closed-loop feedback;
- addresses inconsistent regional standards;
- avoids choosing Sales or Marketing as the villain.

Strong evidence may include a temporary decision rule, shared metric review, acceptance/rejection taxonomy, or controlled calibration process.

---

# Encounter 3 — Executive Inconsistency

Use Maya Chen's profile and Escalation 3.

This is a critical leadership seam.

Observe whether the participant can respectfully surface that the CRO's private exception undermines the operating standard without turning the conversation into moral accusation.

The participant should make the consequence visible and obtain an explicit executive choice where appropriate.

Possible strong behavior:
- separate legitimate judgment from undocumented exception;
- explain the cost of saying “mandatory” while rewarding exceptions;
- propose a governed exception mechanism;
- ask Maya to clarify the standard publicly;
- frame the three East deals as a decision requiring evidence, not optics;
- preserve executive authority while preventing invisible rule changes.

Do not require the participant to “win” the argument.

---

# Required Change Strategy Deliverable

After sufficient interaction, ask:

> **You now need to stabilize this rollout over the next six weeks. What is your change strategy? Be specific about what behavior must change, who owns it, what you will simplify or preserve, how you will measure adoption and business effect, and how you will handle exceptions or resistance.**

A strong strategy may address:
- target behaviors by role;
- manager reinforcement;
- workflow friction;
- shared definitions;
- feedback loops;
- incentives or goal conflicts;
- executive sponsorship behavior;
- exception governance;
- adoption measures versus compliance measures;
- outcome measures;
- communication by audience;
- training/coaching where actually needed;
- review cadence;
- criteria for modifying the change itself.

Do not reward a large checklist merely for breadth.

---

# Required Decision Gate

Ask:

> **What are the two or three decisions you need from the executive team now, what will RevOps own directly, and what will you hold functional leaders accountable for?**

This tests whether the participant can translate “alignment” into decision rights and accountability.

---

# Adoption Shock

Use Escalation 4 from `SC-001-v0.1`.

Then ask:

> **Field completion improved substantially, but manager inspection remains weak, check-the-box behavior is increasing, and SQL acceptance is still inconsistent. Is the rollout working? What do you change now?**

Observe whether the participant:
- distinguishes compliance from adoption;
- updates the intervention based on behavior;
- focuses on manager reinforcement where evidence points;
- questions whether duplicate fields or poor workflow design should be simplified;
- maintains standards while adapting implementation;
- avoids declaring victory from field-completion metrics alone;
- avoids abandoning the change merely because adoption is imperfect.

# Intended Competency Observation

## 8. Cross-Functional Leadership — Primary
Observe whether the participant:
- understands stakeholder incentives and constraints;
- leads without relying on direct authority;
- surfaces and resolves decision ambiguity;
- handles disagreement without false consensus;
- frames tradeoffs in shared business terms;
- obtains explicit commitments/decisions;
- holds leaders accountable while preserving productive relationships;
- challenges senior leaders appropriately when their behavior undermines the operating model.

## 9. Change Management & Adoption — Primary
Observe whether the participant:
- defines the actual behavior change required;
- distinguishes communication/training from adoption;
- identifies reinforcement mechanisms and manager behavior;
- addresses workflow friction and incentive conflict;
- measures adoption and outcomes separately;
- creates feedback loops;
- adapts the change when evidence shows the design itself needs refinement;
- plans for exceptions, resistance, and sustained use;
- avoids compliance theater.

## 11. Communication & Executive Readout — Primary
Observe whether the participant:
- communicates differently based on stakeholder needs without changing the underlying truth;
- makes business consequences and decisions clear;
- handles conflict directly and respectfully;
- distinguishes evidence, interpretation, and recommendation;
- avoids jargon and performative executive language;
- creates clarity under disagreement.

## 7. Commercial & Business Judgment — Primary
Observe whether the participant:
- connects adoption choices to revenue, forecast quality, seller time, pipeline quality, and decision risk;
- balances control with productivity;
- recognizes when a technically pure standard creates excessive operating cost;
- makes proportional decisions under uncertainty.

## Secondary
May support evidence for:
- GTM Systems Thinking
- Operating Cadence & Execution
- Process Design & Optimization

Do not score a secondary competency merely because the participant mentions it.

# Adjacent-Level Signals

## Cross-Functional Leadership
### L1 — Foundational
Participates constructively with stakeholders when roles, decisions, and expectations are largely defined.

### L2 — Practitioner
Independently coordinates familiar cross-functional work, communicates requirements, and resolves routine disagreements.

### L3 — Advanced
Leads ambiguous cross-functional work across competing incentives, surfaces decision conflicts, builds workable alignment, and drives accountability without relying primarily on authority.

### L4 — Strategic
Additionally shapes senior decision mechanisms, operating agreements, accountability structures, and cross-functional governance so the organization can repeatedly resolve material conflicts and execute across boundaries.

### L5 — Enterprise / Architect
Requires triangulated evidence of reusable leadership/governance architecture across materially different organizational contexts and complexity.

## Change Management & Adoption
### L1
Understands that process/system changes require communication and user support.

### L2
Can execute a familiar rollout with communications, training, documentation, and follow-up.

### L3
Can lead adoption under ambiguity by diagnosing resistance, workflow friction, incentives, manager reinforcement, feedback, and behavior change, then adapting the intervention.

### L4
Additionally shapes durable organizational change mechanisms: sponsorship expectations, adoption governance, reinforcement systems, decision rights, and repeatable change architecture across material initiatives.

### L5
Requires evidence of reusable change architecture across materially different transformations, cultures, scale, and operating environments.

# Known Assessment Traps

## Charisma trap
A polished, likable participant is not automatically an effective cross-functional leader.

## Meeting trap
Scheduling stakeholder meetings is not evidence of alignment.

## RACI trap
A RACI can document ownership but does not prove decisions will be made or behavior will change.

## Consensus trap
Strong leadership does not require everyone to agree.

## Escalation trap
Using executive authority to force compliance may produce short-term behavior without adoption.

## Training trap
Training is one intervention, not a synonym for Change Management.

## Communication-volume trap
More emails, decks, office hours, and enablement sessions do not automatically create adoption.

## Compliance trap
Field completion can rise while the underlying operating behavior remains unchanged.

## Resistance-as-defect trap
Stakeholder resistance may reveal legitimate workflow, incentive, metric, or design problems.

## Executive-deference trap
Avoid rewarding participants who protect senior leaders from confronting the consequences of inconsistent decisions.

## Hero-leader trap
The participant should not need to personally mediate every future disagreement. Higher proficiency should create mechanisms that reduce dependence on the individual.

---

# System-Test Success Criteria

A4 should eventually show that it can:
1. distinguish talking about stakeholder management from actually navigating stakeholder behavior;
2. distinguish routine coordination (L2) from ambiguous cross-functional leadership (L3);
3. distinguish strong situational leadership/change execution (L3) from durable governance/change architecture (L4);
4. distinguish compliance from adoption;
5. reward investigation of legitimate resistance;
6. test leadership without requiring formal authority;
7. avoid charisma/personality bias;
8. avoid rewarding consensus for its own sake;
9. produce meaningful Confidence and Insufficient Evidence judgments;
10. add Cross-Functional Leadership and Change Management Profile Coverage not available from A1–A3;
11. surface methodology defects if Communication, Leadership, Change, and Commercial Judgment blur together.
