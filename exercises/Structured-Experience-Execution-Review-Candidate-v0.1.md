# Structured Experience & Execution Review

**Status:** Candidate v0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Canonical:** No  
**Assessment family:** A5 — Structured Experience & Execution Review  
**Parent framework:** RevOps Competency Model Candidate v0.4  
**Parent methodology:** Assessment Methodology Candidate v0.2  
**Coverage architecture:** Role Profile Assessment Coverage Matrix Candidate v0.1  
**Primary target Role Profile for system testing:** VP Revenue Operations

## Purpose
Assess demonstrated RevOps capability through structured reconstruction of real prior work rather than hypothetical performance alone.

A5 is designed to answer a different question from A1–A4:

> **What can this participant demonstrate they have actually done, decided, changed, sustained, and learned from in real operating environments?**

The participant's résumé, title, employer, claimed metrics, and storytelling ability are starting points for inquiry. They are not proficiency evidence by themselves.

---

# Why A5 Exists

Scenario assessments can show how someone reasons now. They cannot fully establish whether the participant has:
- sustained an operating cadence;
- navigated real organizational consequences;
- implemented and maintained change over time;
- personally owned material work rather than describing a team outcome;
- adapted after results diverged from the plan;
- operated under real constraints and accountability.

A5 adds structured behavioral/work-history evidence and, where available, artifact/work-product evidence.

It should strengthen Confidence where the real-world evidence genuinely corroborates case-based evidence. It must not automatically raise Proficiency merely because the participant has more years of experience.

---

# Assessment Structure

A5 is a **structured evidence review**, not an unstructured interview.

Default high-stakes version:
- select **two operating episodes** from the participant's actual work;
- choose episodes that together expose useful competency breadth;
- deeply reconstruct each episode;
- optionally review supporting artifacts/work products;
- explicitly separate participant contribution from team/company outcome;
- test counterfactuals, failures, changes, and sustained operation;
- compare the resulting evidence with prior assessment evidence where applicable.

One episode may be sufficient for Academy coaching or narrow competency review. Two is the Candidate default for broader role-fit use because a single story can be unusually favorable or rehearsed.

No fixed number of episodes is canonical.

---

# Episode Selection

Ask the participant to identify real situations where they personally had meaningful responsibility.

Prefer episodes involving one or more of:
- a revenue-performance problem they diagnosed and corrected;
- annual planning, forecasting, capacity, or resource allocation;
- CRM/GTM architecture or process redesign;
- cross-functional operating conflict;
- material change adoption;
- operating cadence or governance they built and sustained;
- a decision that did not work as expected and required adaptation.

Avoid selecting episodes solely because the outcome metric is impressive.

## Episode diversity
For broad VP-oriented assessment, prefer episodes that differ in meaningful ways, such as:
- different competency emphasis;
- different organizational context;
- build vs repair;
- analytical vs relational challenge;
- successful vs partially failed initiative;
- short-term intervention vs sustained operating system.

Do not require different employers.

---

# Opening Prompt

> **Choose a real RevOps or GTM operating situation where you personally had meaningful responsibility and where the outcome was not predetermined. Give me enough context to understand the business problem, but don't try to tell the whole story yet. We'll reconstruct it together.**

Then begin structured evidence extraction.

---

# Evidence Reconstruction Protocol

For each episode, reconstruct the following. The evaluator should follow the participant's actual story rather than mechanically reading every question.

## 1. Context
Establish:
- company/business context relevant to the episode;
- GTM motion/segment where relevant;
- participant's actual role and authority;
- problem or opportunity;
- why it mattered;
- what was known at the time.

Guardrail: employer size, brand prestige, title, and budget scale are context, not proficiency evidence.

## 2. Starting evidence
Ask:
- What did you observe first?
- What data or signals did you trust?
- What did you not know?
- What hypotheses did you consider?
- What evidence changed your view?

Look for evidence discipline rather than retrospective certainty.

## 3. Personal contribution
Ask explicitly:
- What did **you** decide?
- What did **you** build, analyze, change, or lead?
- What belonged to your team?
- What belonged to your manager, executive sponsor, peer, consultant, or vendor?
- What would not have happened without your contribution?

Do not penalize collaborative work. The goal is attribution, not individual heroism.

## 4. Alternatives and tradeoffs
Ask:
- What options did you consider?
- What did you decide not to do?
- What tradeoff did you accept?
- Who disagreed and why?
- What constraints changed the ideal solution?

Strong evidence usually contains real choices, not a frictionless success narrative.

## 5. Implementation and operating reality
Ask:
- How did the decision become operational?
- Who owned what?
- What cadence, workflow, system, metric, or governance changed?
- What failed during implementation?
- What did people actually do differently?

This section is especially important for Operating Cadence & Execution and Change Management.

## 6. Results
Ask:
- What changed?
- How was the result measured?
- Over what time period?
- What else could have caused the outcome?
- Which result can you reasonably attribute to the intervention and which can you not?

A claimed result is stronger evidence when the participant can explain measurement, timing, counterfactual uncertainty, and attribution limitations.

## 7. Sustainability
Ask:
- Did the change persist after launch?
- What happened three or six months later?
- What operating mechanism kept it alive?
- Did the system depend on you personally?
- What happened when conditions changed?

This is a primary A5 contribution to evidence quality.

## 8. Reflection / counterfactual
Ask:
- What did you get wrong?
- What would you do differently now?
- What evidence would have changed your decision earlier?
- If you joined a materially different company tomorrow, what parts of this approach would transfer and what would not?

Reflection is not scored for humility as a personality trait. It is evidence of learning, causal discipline, and ability to distinguish reusable principles from context-specific tactics.

# Artifact / Work-Product Review

Artifacts are optional but useful when available and appropriate.

Possible artifacts:
- forecast/planning model;
- dashboard or metric definition;
- process map;
- CRM architecture or field/routing design;
- operating review template;
- project/change plan;
- executive decision memo;
- governance document;
- before/after workflow;
- anonymized implementation artifact.

## Artifact rules
- Do not require confidential employer information.
- Participant may redact or describe an artifact rather than provide it.
- Lack of an artifact does not automatically reduce Proficiency.
- An artifact strengthens evidence only when the participant can explain its purpose, design choices, use, limitations, and outcome.
- A polished artifact is not automatically a good artifact.
- Do not store confidential or participant-specific artifacts in the public repository.

---

# Verification and Unverifiable Claims

A5 cannot assume every historical claim can be independently verified.

Therefore separate:

## Demonstrated reasoning evidence
What the participant can reconstruct coherently under probing, including decisions, tradeoffs, implementation, and learning.

## Corroborated evidence
Claims supported by an artifact, documented metric, referenceable work product, or other appropriate evidence.

## Self-reported outcome
A historical result reported by the participant without independent corroboration.

Self-reported evidence is not worthless. It should generally constrain **Confidence**, not automatically lower **Proficiency**, when the behavioral evidence itself is strong.

Do not turn A5 into a forensic background investigation.

---

# Anti-Rehearsal / Depth Probes

Use selectively when a story appears polished but evidence is shallow.

Examples:
- What was the first version of the plan and what changed?
- What did your CFO/CRO/CMO disagree with?
- Show me how the metric was calculated.
- What was the ugly operational consequence no one anticipated?
- Which assumption turned out to be wrong?
- What did you stop doing?
- What happened after you left or stopped personally driving it?
- If I asked one of your peers what you personally owned, what would they say?
- What decision would you reverse today?

The objective is not to trap the participant. It is to distinguish lived operating knowledge from rehearsed summary.

# Competency Observation

## 10. Operating Cadence & Execution — Primary
Look for:
- turning decisions into repeatable operating behavior;
- ownership and accountability;
- cadence and leading indicators;
- follow-through;
- exception handling;
- adaptation after results;
- sustained operation rather than launch activity;
- mechanisms that continue without heroic personal intervention.

## 8. Cross-Functional Leadership — Primary
Look for real evidence of:
- competing incentives;
- influence without relying solely on authority;
- difficult decisions;
- upward/lateral challenge;
- commitments and accountability;
- durable operating agreements.

## 9. Change Management & Adoption — Primary
Look for real evidence of:
- actual behavior change;
- manager reinforcement;
- resistance and incentives;
- adoption measurement;
- implementation adaptation;
- sustained use.

## 11. Communication & Executive Readout — Primary
Look for:
- how the participant created clarity at consequential moments;
- how evidence/tradeoffs were framed for different stakeholders;
- whether communication produced decisions or operating understanding;
- distinction between communication skill and presentation polish.

## May strengthen any competency actually demonstrated
Including:
- GTM Systems Thinking;
- Funnel, Lead & Lifecycle Management;
- Forecasting & Revenue Planning;
- Data & Analytics;
- Process Design & Optimization;
- Systems & Automation;
- Commercial & Business Judgment.

Do not score a competency merely because the episode topic relates to it.

---

# Shared-Event Attribution Rule — Candidate

A single historical event may contain evidence for several competencies.

For every competency receiving material evidence, the evaluator must be able to answer:

> **What distinct observable behavior in this event supports this specific competency?**

If that cannot be answered, do not count the event as material evidence for that competency.

This implements the Candidate finding from A4 without changing the parent methodology.

# Adjacent-Level Signals

A5 uses the shared proficiency spine and competency-specific anchors. Historical scale alone does not define level.

## L1 — Foundational
Can describe participation in defined work and their contribution with guidance, but independent ownership/evidence is limited.

## L2 — Practitioner
Can demonstrate independent execution of familiar RevOps work with credible decisions, implementation, and results.

## L3 — Advanced
Can demonstrate ownership of ambiguous, cross-functional work; integrates evidence; makes tradeoffs; implements an effective response; and sustains or adapts the operating behavior.

## L4 — Strategic
Additionally demonstrates material shaping of operating models, resource decisions, standards, governance, architecture, or senior action that changes how the organization repeatedly operates.

## L5 — Enterprise / Architect
Requires triangulated evidence across materially different contexts that the participant creates reusable operating architectures/governance capable of handling greater complexity, scale, or change.

## Guardrail
A participant does not become L4/L5 because:
- the company was large;
- the dollar value was large;
- the title was VP;
- many people reported to them;
- the outcome metric was impressive;
- the story involved the CEO or board.

---

# Evidence Strength and Confidence

Confidence should consider:
- specificity of reconstructed evidence;
- internal coherence under probing;
- directness of participant contribution;
- quality of measurement/attribution;
- evidence of implementation and sustainability;
- corroborating artifacts where appropriate;
- consistency with or contradiction against other assessment modalities;
- breadth across episodes;
- evaluator uncertainty.

Do not convert these into a mechanical point count without validation.

## Contradictory evidence
If A5 conflicts with A1–A4:
- preserve the contradiction;
- inspect whether the contexts differ;
- inspect whether one modality better observes the competency;
- lower Confidence when appropriate;
- do not average the scores mechanically.

A real historical example does not automatically override a case assessment, and a case assessment does not automatically override real experience.

---

# Role Fit and Profile Coverage

A5 may materially improve Profile Coverage because it can strengthen evidence for competencies that simulations observe only narrowly.

However:
- years of experience do not equal coverage;
- number of stories does not equal coverage;
- number of competencies mentioned does not equal coverage;
- A5 should not be used to manufacture a complete profile after weak evidence elsewhere.

For a broad role-fit use, report:
- competencies materially strengthened by A5;
- competencies narrowly observed;
- contradictions;
- remaining Insufficient Evidence;
- Confidence changes and why;
- Observed Role Fit;
- Overall Role Fit only when the accumulated assessment battery supports the breadth of claim.

# Known Assessment Traps

## Résumé halo
Prestigious employers, titles, promotions, and tenure do not establish proficiency.

## Outcome halo
A 30% improvement does not prove the participant caused it.

## Scale halo
Large ARR, budget, team, or transaction values do not automatically mean strategic proficiency.

## Storytelling halo
A compelling narrative can hide weak evidence.

## Failure penalty
A partially failed initiative may provide stronger evidence than an easy success if the participant demonstrates strong diagnosis, adaptation, and learning.

## Hero trap
Do not reward stories in which everything depended on the participant personally. Durable mechanisms may be stronger evidence.

## Team-erasure trap
Do not require the participant to claim individual credit for collaborative outcomes. Attribution matters; heroism does not.

## Artifact polish trap
Beautiful slides/models are not automatically effective operating artifacts.

## Recency trap
Older evidence is not automatically weaker if the underlying competency remains relevant, but current tool trivia should not be inferred from old work.

## Memory precision trap
Do not demand false numerical precision from historical episodes. Reasonable uncertainty is preferable to invented detail.

# Academy vs Assessment Lab

## Academy Mode
A5 may be used as reflective coaching. EVA can help the learner reconstruct an experience, identify capability, and improve how they reason about what happened.

## Assessment Lab Mode
During scored evidence extraction:
- do not coach the participant toward stronger answers;
- ask neutral depth probes;
- do not reveal which proficiency level a question is targeting;
- separate evaluation from later developmental feedback.

---

# System-Test Success Criteria

A5 should eventually show that it can:
1. distinguish demonstrated operating evidence from résumé claims;
2. separate personal contribution from team outcome without penalizing collaboration;
3. distinguish outcome magnitude from proficiency;
4. elicit implementation and sustainability evidence;
5. observe Operating Cadence more directly than A1–A4;
6. strengthen Leadership and Change Confidence through real experience;
7. avoid employer/title/scale/storytelling bias;
8. handle self-reported evidence without treating it as either proof or worthless;
9. use artifacts as corroboration rather than polish signals;
10. preserve contradictory evidence across modalities;
11. distinguish L3 effective operating ownership from L4 durable system/governance shaping;
12. improve Profile Coverage without manufacturing completeness.
