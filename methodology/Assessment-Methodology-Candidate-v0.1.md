# RevOps Academy & Assessment Lab — Assessment Methodology

**Status:** Candidate v0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Canonical:** No  
**Parent framework:** RevOps Competency Model Candidate v0.4  
**Purpose:** Consolidate tested assessment principles into a coherent methodology for obtaining, interpreting, and recording evidence of RevOps capability.

## Scope
This methodology answers:

> **What evidence would allow us to make a trustworthy judgment about demonstrated RevOps capability?**

It does not redefine the Competency Model, proficiency levels, or Role Profiles.

The approved Candidate-development architecture remains:

> **Competency × Proficiency × Role Profile**

Assessment Methodology sits beneath that model and governs how evidence is collected and interpreted.

---

# Assessment Architecture

The working assessment stack is:

> **Competency Model → Assessment Methodology → Exercise / Assessment Instrument → Evidence Record / Evaluation**

A reusable **Dataset** may provide the operating reality referenced by an Exercise.

## Competency Model
Defines:
- what capability is being evaluated;
- the shared L1–L5 proficiency altitude;
- Role Profile expectations;
- competency-specific evidence anchors by reference.

## Assessment Methodology
Defines:
- evidence quality and sufficiency;
- evidence modalities;
- dynamic evidence rules;
- Confidence;
- triangulation;
- evaluator discipline and consistency;
- fairness/comparability principles;
- Insufficient evidence rules;
- separation of proficiency from Role Fit.

## Exercise / Assessment Instrument
Defines:
- scenario and task;
- dataset/version where applicable;
- prompts and interaction sequence;
- stakeholder roles;
- evidence-release mechanics;
- selected evidence modality or combination;
- competencies intended to be observable;
- evaluator instructions by reference.

## Evidence Record / Evaluation
Records:
- what the participant actually demonstrated;
- competency-specific evidence;
- proficiency judgment;
- Confidence;
- Role Fit comparison where applicable;
- Insufficient evidence;
- evaluator rationale.

# Four Separate Assessment Questions

Every assessment record should preserve four distinct judgments:

1. **Competency:** What capability was demonstrated?
2. **Proficiency:** At what level was that capability demonstrated?
3. **Role Fit:** How does the demonstrated profile compare with the target Role Profile?
4. **Confidence:** How strong, broad, and repeatable is the evidence supporting the judgment?

Do not collapse these into a single score by default.

A participant can demonstrate L4 proficiency with Moderate Confidence. A participant can demonstrate L2 with High Confidence. A participant can meet a Role Profile without receiving the same level in every competency.

---

# Evidence Standard

## Demonstrated evidence over presumed capability
Score what the participant demonstrates.

Do not infer proficiency from:
- title;
- years of experience;
- employer size;
- résumé claims;
- certifications;
- confidence or charisma;
- sophisticated vocabulary;
- tool breadth;
- organizational authority.

## Evidence chain
A useful cross-cutting reasoning lens is:

> **Evidence → Interpretation → Economics → Tradeoff → Decision → Accountability**

Not every competency or exercise must demonstrate every link. This is an evaluator lens, not a prescribed participant response format.

## Candidate cross-cutting evidence dimensions
These may inform judgments across several competencies but are **not additional competencies**:

1. Evidence Discipline
2. Causal Discipline
3. Economic Connection
4. Tradeoff Quality
5. Decision Quality
6. Uncertainty Handling
7. Accountability & Implementation Logic

Do not score these in a way that double-counts the same behavior already captured within a competency.

# Evidence Quality Guardrails

## Unsupported causal certainty
Confidently asserting causation without sufficient evidence should constrain advanced proficiency where causal reasoning matters.

## Fabricated facts
Inventing missing facts is an Evidence Discipline failure.

## Vocabulary inflation
Terminology has no independent scoring value.

## Unbounded analysis
Requesting every conceivable metric without prioritizing decision-relevant evidence is not analytical sophistication.

## Transformation bias
Major structural change should not be rewarded merely because it sounds strategic. Evidence should support the scale and reversibility of the proposed action.

## Healthy uncertainty
Recognizing missing evidence, requesting information that could change a decision, and avoiding unsupported irreversible action are positive signals.

Healthy uncertainty becomes a limitation when sufficient evidence exists for a proportional/reversible decision and the participant remains unable to decide.

## Hypothesis revision
Changing a hypothesis when contradictory evidence appears is positive evidence, not a scoring failure.

# Evidence Modality

**Evidence modality** is the method by which observable evidence is elicited or collected.

Modality belongs primarily in Assessment Methodology and is selected/implemented by individual Exercises. It is **not** a fourth dimension of Competency × Proficiency × Role Profile.

Candidate modalities include:

1. Analytical Case
2. Dynamic Diagnostic Case
3. Technical / System-Design Case
4. Stakeholder Simulation
5. Structured Behavioral / Work-History Evidence
6. Artifact / Work-Product Review
7. Live Task / Demonstration
8. Longitudinal Observation

The taxonomy may evolve without changing the Competency Model.

## Many-to-many relationship
One competency may require several modalities for high Confidence. One modality may produce evidence for several competencies.

Therefore, modality is an evidence-collection design choice, not participant capability.

## Modality and Confidence
A stronger modality does not automatically produce a higher proficiency score.

Use stronger or multiple modalities to increase Confidence in the proficiency judgment when appropriate.

Example: an excellent written answer may support L4 Cross-Functional Leadership reasoning with Moderate Confidence; repeated stakeholder simulations plus structured work-history evidence may support the same L4 judgment with higher Confidence.

---

# Evidence Sufficiency and Insufficient Evidence

## Insufficient evidence is not low proficiency
If an assessment does not meaningfully expose a competency, record:

> **Insufficient evidence**

Do not force an L1 or L2 score.

## Missing hidden evidence
A participant should not be penalized for failing to uncover facts they had no decision-relevant reason to request.

Missing evidence may constrain the scope of claims or the competencies that can be scored. It should not automatically reduce proficiency.

## Minimum evidence
A proficiency judgment requires enough observable evidence to distinguish the assigned level from adjacent levels with a defensible rationale.

A single phrase, keyword, or generic statement is not sufficient evidence of advanced proficiency.

Exact minimum evidence requirements may vary by competency and remain an open validation question.

---

# Dynamic Evidence Method

Dynamic assessments may reveal information in response to participant questions and decisions.

The hidden evidence environment is **responsive context, not an answer key**.

A strong dynamic path may demonstrate:
1. identifying the real decision or problem;
2. requesting high-value evidence;
3. forming a bounded hypothesis;
4. revising that hypothesis when evidence changes;
5. connecting evidence to consequences and tradeoffs;
6. recognizing when enough evidence exists for a proportional decision;
7. assigning ownership or governance appropriate to the role.

## No packet-count scoring
Discovering more evidence packets is not inherently better.

A participant who requests four consequential pieces of evidence and reaches a defensible decision may demonstrate stronger prioritization than someone who requests eight indiscriminately.

## Multiple valid inquiry paths
Participants may enter a problem through funnel, economics, capacity, process, systems, customer, or other defensible lenses.

Equivalent reasoning quality should be capable of receiving equivalent Role Fit treatment even when the resulting competency evidence profiles differ.

## Score encountered evidence
Do not award credit for hidden mechanisms the participant never investigated and do not penalize the participant for not mentioning undisclosed facts.

## Disconfirmation
A plausible initial hypothesis followed by evidence-driven revision is positive evidence of Causal Discipline and Uncertainty Handling.

## Decision threshold
Evaluators should observe the point at which enough evidence exists for a bounded, reversible, role-appropriate action.

Do not reduce this to a universal number of questions or evidence packets.

# Proficiency Judgment

Use the shared L1–L5 spine from the current Competency Model Candidate and competency-specific evidence anchors.

The evaluator should ask:

> **What observable behavior demonstrates the transition from the adjacent lower level to the level being assigned in this competency?**

## Diagnosis versus system shaping
A recurring L3/L4 calibration issue is the difference between diagnosing/designing a strong response and materially shaping how the system operates.

In competencies where strategic responsibility is inherent:
- **L3** commonly integrates ambiguous evidence, diagnoses cross-functional causes, challenges assumptions, and designs an effective response;
- **L4** additionally shapes a material operating decision, tradeoff, standard, governance mechanism, architecture, or senior-stakeholder action appropriate to that competency.

This is a calibration principle, not a requirement that every L4 response contain an explicit action sentence.

## Do not average adjacent disagreement
If evaluators disagree between levels, identify the specific boundary behavior in dispute. Do not resolve disagreement by arithmetic averaging.

# Confidence

Candidate Confidence labels:

## Low Confidence
Isolated, ambiguous, narrow, or heavily self-reported evidence.

## Moderate Confidence
Multiple consistent signals with limited breadth, repetition, direct observation, or contextual variety.

## High Confidence
Repeated clear evidence across materially different situations and/or strong triangulation appropriate to the competency.

High Confidence means confidence in the judgment, not high proficiency.

## Confidence should reflect evidence quality, not evidence count
Several weak sources do not automatically create strong triangulation.

## Confidence can increase without changing proficiency
Additional evidence may confirm an existing L3 judgment and move Confidence from Low to High without changing the proficiency level.

---

# Triangulation

Do not require multiple modalities for every competency or every Academy exercise.

Evidence burden should increase as:
- the stakes increase;
- the breadth of the proficiency claim increases;
- the competency is difficult to observe in a single interaction;
- self-report constitutes a large share of evidence;
- the decision has significant hiring, certification, or development consequences.

Candidate principle:

> **Evidence burden should rise with the consequence and breadth of the claim, not merely with the participant's title.**

A low-stakes Academy exercise may reasonably produce a provisional L3 / Moderate Confidence judgment. A high-stakes claim of durable L5 Cross-Functional Leadership should require broader evidence.

---

# Competency Coverage and Construct Separation

One assessment may produce evidence for several competencies, but evaluators must identify the **distinct competency-specific behavior** being scored.

Examples:
- strong commercial judgment does not prove strong Cross-Functional Leadership;
- clear executive communication does not prove leadership;
- technical implementation depth does not prove systems governance;
- successful adoption does not automatically prove the original cross-functional decision process was strong;
- a good operating cadence does not prove Commercial Judgment;
- mentioning stakeholders does not create Cross-Functional Leadership evidence.

Avoid competency contamination and hidden double counting.

---

# Role Fit

Role Fit compares the observed competency profile with the target Role Profile. It does not change the underlying proficiency scores.

Candidate labels:
- Below target
- Approaching target
- Meets target
- Exceeds target
- Insufficient evidence

Role Fit should be explainable from the observed profile and should not be represented as a complete hiring decision.

A participant may meet a Role Profile with meaningful variation across competencies.

# Evaluator Discipline and Consistency

## Evidence before interpretation
The evaluator record should distinguish:
1. observable participant evidence;
2. evaluator interpretation;
3. proficiency judgment;
4. Confidence;
5. Role Fit comparison.

## Cite the boundary
For material proficiency judgments, especially adjacent levels, evaluators should identify the behavior that separates the assigned level from the adjacent lower/higher level.

## No preferred hidden path
Evaluators must not score participants against a secret preferred inquiry sequence.

## No title calibration
Do not increase or decrease proficiency because the participant is known to be an Analyst, Manager, Director, VP, consultant, or executive.

Role title affects the Role Profile comparison, not the demonstrated proficiency judgment.

## Evaluator disagreement
When evaluators disagree:
- identify the exact evidence and boundary rule causing disagreement;
- do not average levels;
- preserve disagreement when evidence does not support resolution;
- use calibration artifacts or an additional independent review when stakes justify it.

## Validation status
Pseudo-independent testing has exposed plausible L3/L4 disagreement. Real inter-rater reliability has **not** been established.

Do not describe the methodology as empirically validated.

# Academy Mode vs Assessment Lab Mode

## Academy Mode
Primary purpose: learning and capability development.

The evaluator may:
- coach after a scored segment;
- explain reasoning gaps;
- show stronger alternatives;
- recommend practice;
- use provisional scoring to guide development.

## Assessment Lab Mode
Primary purpose: disciplined evaluation.

The evaluator should:
- preserve the intended evidence environment;
- avoid coaching during a scored segment unless response to feedback is intentionally being assessed;
- distinguish observation from interpretation;
- record evidence before revealing scoring logic where appropriate;
- maintain consistent interaction rules across comparable participants.

The competency architecture is shared. Interaction rules differ by mode.

# Candidate Evidence Record

A reusable evaluation record should eventually capture:

- participant or anonymized participant ID where appropriate;
- exercise and version;
- dataset and version where applicable;
- target Role Profile;
- evidence modality/modalities;
- competencies intended to be observed;
- competencies actually observed;
- evidence excerpts or structured notes;
- proficiency judgment by observed competency;
- Confidence by judgment;
- Role Fit comparison where applicable;
- material strengths;
- material risks;
- Insufficient evidence areas;
- evaluator identity/version where appropriate;
- unresolved evaluator disagreement.

Participant-specific or confidential assessment records should not be stored in the public repository.

# Hiring and Certification Guardrails

## Hiring
Assessment evidence may inform hiring but is not a complete hiring decision.

Hiring may legitimately include experience, domain context, leadership fit, values, compensation, references, role-specific requirements, and evidence outside this framework.

## Certification
This methodology is not yet a validated certification standard.

Do not establish certification thresholds, pass/fail claims, or externally validated capability claims until evidence supports them and JET explicitly approves the applicable canonical methodology.

---

# Current Validation Support

Candidate-level testing currently supports:
- a shared five-level proficiency spine across analytical, technical/architectural, and behavioral/relational vertical slices;
- competency-specific evidence anchors;
- dynamic inquiry with multiple valid paths;
- Insufficient evidence as a fairness mechanism;
- Confidence separate from proficiency;
- Role Fit separate from proficiency;
- evidence modality primarily in Assessment Methodology;
- stronger modality affecting Confidence rather than automatically raising proficiency;
- hypothesis revision as positive evidence;
- a decision threshold for higher-altitude work;
- anti-inflation rules for vocabulary, title, tool depth, and organizational scale.

---

# Not Yet Established

The following remain unvalidated or incomplete:
- empirical inter-rater reliability;
- predictive validity for job performance;
- minimum evidence thresholds by competency;
- full L1–L5 evidence anchors for all eleven competencies;
- stable Role Profile weighting or emphasis;
- numeric scoring or composite summaries;
- certification thresholds;
- durability of proficiency judgments over time;
- standardized stakeholder simulation mechanics;
- standardized work-history evidence protocol;
- the appropriate evidence burden for L5 across each competency.
